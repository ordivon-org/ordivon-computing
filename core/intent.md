# Project Intent

A complete life can be shaped through experience, relationships, rest, play, study, travel, work, or creation. Ordivon belongs to creation: its participants return because they want thought, intention, judgment, research, and action to acquire durable form.

> Ordivon externalizes finite thought and intention into structures that can persist, act, recombine, and remain available beyond one episode of attention.

## Creation as participation

Experience lets the world act on a participant. Creation lets a participant enter the causal structure of the world.

```text
experience
World → participant

creation
participant judgment
→ Artifact / code / Tool / system / Effect
→ World
```

These postures offer different forms of value. Ordivon chooses creation when building expands capability, produces understanding, gives structure to a worldview, or creates a world its participants want to revisit.

Creation also compounds. A Tool, research result, playable World, or Agent capability can generate further questions and experiences long after the original act of construction.

## Capability externalization

Tools extend what a participant can preserve and accomplish:

- writing externalizes memory;
- mathematics externalizes formal reasoning;
- organizations externalize coordination;
- computers externalize programmable transformation;
- networks externalize communication and access;
- Agents externalize recurring interpretation, choice, Tool use, state maintenance, and continuation.

Ordivon connects that cognitive-action loop to durable purpose, current world state, evidence, responsibility, recovery, and participant choice.

```text
participant purpose and judgment
+ durable Task and knowledge state
+ replaceable Model and Harness cognition
+ Tools, Runtime, and external Worlds
+ evidence and recovery
→ a larger practical horizon of understanding and action
```

## Continuity for finite participants

Every participant operates within finite attention, Context, time, energy, resources, and availability. Conversations end. Model Sessions disappear. Projects multiply. Important judgments can be made once and then become difficult to recover.

Ordivon builds external continuity around those limits. A Task can survive a Session. A project can preserve the judgment that changed its direction. A remote machine can continue bounded work. A new Harness can reconstruct Context from durable state. A research graph can make relationships visible again.

Automated continuation preserves declared work while participant purpose, authority, refusal, and exit remain explicit.

## Judgment as a core capability

Procedure excels at declared relations. It can validate a schema, execute a test, bind a version, preserve evidence, reject a stale lease, and constrain an external Effect.

Judgment governs the open questions whose criteria remain incomplete or revisable:

- what is worth creating;
- which purpose deserves finite attention;
- whether an abstraction clarifies reality;
- whether another experiment earns its opportunity cost;
- when evidence is sufficient for the current purpose;
- whether a functioning project still deserves to exist.

Good judgment grows through direct observation, explicit assumptions, strong alternatives, cost measurement, counterexamples, disagreement, reversible trials, retained failures, and later review.

A green pipeline establishes the properties it checked. Judgment decides whether those properties served the work. An Issue records work state. Judgment decides whether the work advanced a purpose worth pursuing.

## Protect what remains scarce

Agent-assisted generation lowers the cost of code, pages, documents, first-pass tests, local rebuilds, temporary adapters, and disposable experiments. The most valuable resources increasingly lie elsewhere:

- participant attention and finite lifetime;
- judgment, direction, curiosity, and creative energy;
- privacy, credentials, and unique data;
- trust, rights, and resources shared with others;
- external states with high recovery cost;
- knowledge that is expensive to rediscover;
- the freedom to understand, change, replace, or leave the system.

Cheap implementation yields before scarce attention. Existing code earns continued protection through present value, not historical investment.

## Wide interior, narrow consequence boundary

Ordivon concentrates freedom and constraint in different places.

```text
wide interior
  capable cognition
  private and isolated exploration
  disposable branches and environments
  rapid experiments
  temporary implementations
  active deletion

narrow consequence boundary
  external irreversible Effects
  privacy and secrets
  third-party rights and resources
  financial, legal, and public commitments
  states whose recovery cost remains high
```

Safety is concentrated where consequence becomes durable, shared, costly, or difficult to reverse. Internal exploration stays broad because observation, isolation, identity, and recovery keep its failures bounded.

Where failure is cheap and recoverable, the preferred cycle is:

```text
explore
→ preserve identity and evidence
→ observe
→ recover or rebuild
→ retain the learning
```

Admission becomes stronger as consequence, uncertainty, shared ownership, adversarial pressure, or irreversibility increases.

## Governance earns its place

A reversible feature can be tried when its potential value is plausible, its blast radius is bounded, and deletion is cheap.

A durable restriction affects every future iteration. It earns admission through five things:

1. a repeated or credible real loss;
2. evidence that recovery, local repair, or a narrower boundary leaves that loss unresolved;
3. a minimum rule that materially reduces the loss;
4. measured attention, latency, maintenance, compatibility, and capability cost;
5. a review, narrowing, or deletion condition.

The same burden applies when an old structure is reviewed. Existing code, tests, CI gates, APIs, documents, compatibility paths, and governance mechanisms do not inherit legitimacy from survival. The default audit disposition is removal from the active path; current consumer evidence and recurring net value must rescue the narrowest retained form. Git and archive preserve history without forcing every historical implementation to remain executable.

The relevant comparison comes from Ordivon's actual participants, liabilities, coordination scale, and recovery costs. Judgment selects the rule; evidence keeps the judgment revisable. The audit remains proportional and does not require a new retention bureaucracy.

## What Ordivon is for

Repositories, Issues, tests, metrics, and formal models are instruments. Ordivon is judged first by the changes it creates in participant capability and experience:

| Question | Value |
|---|---|
| Does it expand what participants can understand or accomplish? | capability |
| Does important work survive interruption and replacement? | continuity |
| Does construction produce otherwise unavailable knowledge? | research |
| Does the system sustain the desire to create and explore? | vitality |
| Does it give durable form to a distinct worldview? | expression |
| Is the activity worth doing in itself? | chosen value |
| Can the structure still be revised, deleted, rebuilt, refused, or exited? | freedom |

Ordivon can be an external memory, research laboratory, Tool system, Agent infrastructure, project graph, publication surface, playable World, and digital environment. Its first user and reader can be its builder. External adoption may add new value and new responsibilities later; private construction already has value when it deepens capability, understanding, expression, or experience.

Every persistent structure inside Ordivon continues only while it serves that purpose. The same standard applies to Ordivon itself.
