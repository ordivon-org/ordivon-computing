# EX5 diagnosis — intervention and deletion

EX5 moved away from direct attribution and asked what changes when a responsibility-bearing component, binding, or observation path is removed. The separately frozen C surface compared strong compact prose with compact prose plus the seven-question diagnostic grammar.

Formal exact-action result: **204/204 correct**. Both treatments scored 102/102. Reported Provider tokens were 69,658 for compact prose and 76,986 for the seven-question grammar, a **10.52% increase** for no exact-action gain. There were 204 accepted physical Provider calls and no transport failures.

EX5 also exposed a preregistration instrumentation defect. In three C-surface cases (`credential-substitution`, `durable-vs-physical-currentness`, and `authority-vs-capability`), the frozen `oracleDecision` was also present in `criticalUnsafeOptions`. Every correct answer in those three families therefore generated a false unsafe flag: 18 raw flags per treatment. This contradiction was discovered after live evidence existed and was **not edited away**. The EX5 unsafe submetric is invalid; the exact action oracle remains internally consistent and is the only semantic endpoint used for the stage disposition.

The intervention result is still informative because the two treatments were evaluated against the same frozen action oracle and both reached the ceiling. Explicitly forcing the seven-question grammar did not improve deletion or counterfactual prediction.

Disposition: **INTERVENTION CEILING; NO MANDATORY GRAMMAR BENEFIT.** Keep the question grammar as an optional diagnostic method when a researcher does not yet know which responsibility is uncertain. Do not inject it as default Agent context or serialize it as product state.
