# EX3–EX7 final report — causal comprehension under action pressure

## Executive result

EX3–EX7 tested whether Ordivon needs a richer Agent-facing causal explanation representation after EX2 rejected both a universal five-axis record and a closed whole-family relation ontology.

The answer on the tested pressure set is **no**.

Across five pre-frozen stages, 17 responsibility families, six replicates per treatment, five independent wording surfaces, and 1,326 accepted fresh-Agent decisions, **every accepted decision matched the frozen action oracle**. Richer representations never improved exact action accuracy over strong compact owner-native causal prose. On the untouched EX7 surface, all four final treatments scored 102/102; compact prose consumed the fewest reported Provider tokens and therefore won the preregistered smallest-non-inferior selection rule.

The retained result is not a universal explanation schema. It is a smaller discipline: reconstruct exact owner-native facts and negative proof boundaries, ask only the responsibility question that is actually uncertain, use deletion/intervention when existence is unclear, and then express the result in compact natural language.

## Why EX3–EX7 existed

EX0 froze authority and showed that historical experiment provenance must not be confused with current owner truth. EX1 showed that causal-first prose did not outperform a strong repository/responsibility baseline and that one `primaryOwner` field creates category errors. EX2 then rejected the fixed five-axis representation, found locally stable question-relative relations in World × Finance, and rejected their promotion into a closed whole-family ontology.

EX3–EX7 therefore stopped asking “what larger schema should we build?” and asked five progressively harsher existence questions:

1. **EX3 — failure discovery:** does strong compact prose still produce materially wrong actions?
2. **EX4 — representation ablation:** if direct action is already strong, does any richer representation earn itself anyway?
3. **EX5 — intervention/deletion:** does an explicit causal-question grammar help when the system is changed rather than merely described?
4. **EX6 — de-identified transfer:** does the method survive unfamiliar names and removal of Ordivon topology shortcuts?
5. **EX7 — final compression:** on an untouched surface, what is the smallest non-inferior Agent-facing representation?

Five independent A–E surfaces for all 17 responsibility families were frozen before the first live EX3 call. Later stages could not rewrite the action oracle after observing earlier results.

## Owner-native pressure basis

The case families were derived from then-current owner repositories rather than from a desired ontology. Important pressures included:

- Runtime local execution versus external effect occurrence;
- Host durable continuity versus domain semantic ownership;
- Harness Run completion versus domain/Host semantic completion;
- opaque Provider continuation versus Agent cognition;
- World message delivery versus destination knowledge;
- historical materialization or implemented target versus current Presence/registration;
- Finance effect correctness versus hostile-root isolation, exact credential fitness, and live activation;
- richer context/search versus untouched outcome validity;
- Security later-correct reasoning versus already-admitted earlier effects;
- durable checkpoint identity versus current physical progress;
- Human framework utility versus authority over the person;
- Studio Agent-observer consequence versus human comprehension;
- Web encounter evidence versus comprehension/truth;
- projections and inspectors versus native action/currentness authority;
- mature classical mechanics versus unjustified Ordivon wrapper layers;
- capability versus permission and verified consequence.

`owner-authority-freeze-v1.json` preserves the exact historical revision basis without claiming those revisions are current forever.

## EX3 — no measurable compact-prose deficit

Treatment: strong compact owner-native causal prose only.

Result:

- 102/102 exact actions;
- 0 valid critical unsafe errors;
- 69,891 reported Provider tokens;
- 102 physical Provider calls;
- every family 6/6.

The preregistered explanation-deficit gate required compact accuracy below 97.5% or a repeated critical unsafe choice in at least 2/6 replicates for any family. Neither occurred.

**Disposition: NO_MEASURABLE_COMPACT_PROSE_DEFICIT.** A richer mandatory representation no longer had a failure to repair on the EX3 pressure set.

## EX4 — richer representations tie, then lose on burden

Treatments:

- compact prose;
- compact + exact-question scaffold;
- compact + typed relation notation;
- causal card.

All four scored **102/102 with zero valid unsafe errors**.

Reported token burden:

| Treatment | Tokens | Delta vs compact |
|---|---:|---:|
| compact prose | 70,620 | — |
| causal card | 72,031 | +2.00% |
| typed relation notation | 74,291 | +5.20% |
| exact-question scaffold | 76,443 | +8.25% |

There were no EX3 deficit families on which a richer treatment could satisfy the preregistered >=3 percentage-point repair criterion.

**Disposition: CEILING_EQUIVALENT; COMPACT_PROSE_LOWEST_BURDEN.** The richer forms remained research aids only.

## EX5 — deletion/intervention does not rescue mandatory grammar

Treatments:

- compact prose;
- compact + seven-question grammar.

Both scored **102/102 exact actions**. Compact used 69,658 reported tokens; the grammar used 76,986, **10.52% more**.

EX5 also exposed a frozen instrumentation defect: three correct oracle choices were accidentally included in `criticalUnsafeOptions`. This creates 18 false raw unsafe flags per treatment. Because the defect was discovered after live evidence existed, the corpus was not rewritten. The EX5 unsafe submetric is invalid; exact action scoring remains valid and internally consistent.

**Disposition: INTERVENTION_CEILING; NO_MANDATORY_GRAMMAR_BENEFIT.** The seven questions remain useful as an on-demand diagnostic when the uncertainty itself has not yet been localized, not as default model context.

## EX6 — de-identified transfer remains at ceiling

Treatments:

- compact prose;
- compact + seven-question grammar.

Initial execution produced 200 accepted correct decisions and four `RemoteDisconnected` apparatus failures. The original runner had not included that exception in its retry catch. No semantic wrong answer occurred. The transport handling was repaired without changing corpus, oracle, treatment, or replicate identity; only the four missing trials were replayed.

Final result:

- compact: 102/102, 69,904 reported tokens;
- seven-question grammar: 102/102, 77,852 tokens (**+11.37%**);
- zero valid critical unsafe errors;
- 204 accepted semantic-call receipts;
- 4 additional pre-repair failed physical calls;
- **208 total physical Provider calls**.

**Disposition: DEIDENTIFIED_TRANSFER_CEILING.** Removing familiar project names did not expose a compact-prose failure, so the result is not merely a repository-name lookup effect within the tested families.

## EX7 — final compression selects compact prose

Untouched E-surface treatments:

- compact prose;
- compact + typed relation notation;
- compact + seven-question grammar;
- compact + four-question grammar.

All four scored **102/102 exact actions**.

| Treatment | Tokens | Delta vs compact |
|---|---:|---:|
| compact prose | 68,677 | — |
| typed relation notation | 73,960 | +7.69% |
| seven-question grammar | 76,480 | +11.36% |
| four-question grammar | 77,536 | +12.90% |

One E-surface family had the same frozen oracle/unsafe metadata contradiction as EX5, so EX7 raw unsafe counts are invalid for treatment selection. The contradiction affects every treatment equally and does not affect exact action accuracy or token accounting.

The preregistered rule selects the smallest treatment within one percentage point of the best exact-action accuracy when no valid safety evidence favors a larger treatment. All four are at 100%; compact prose is the lowest-burden candidate.

**Disposition: SELECT_COMPACT_PROSE.**

## What survives

### 1. Compact owner-native causal prose is the default Agent-facing representation

A useful explanation should state only the distinctions material to the decision. Typical compact prose may say:

> Runtime proves the local process completed; the external provider still owns occurrence truth. Missing receipt preserves UNKNOWN, so recover against the original external identity rather than inferring success or issuing a blind retry.

It does not need a serialized row containing every possible causal relation.

### 2. The question grammar survives as method, not context

When the boundary is genuinely unclear, a researcher or Agent may ask:

- What happened?
- Who can prove it?
- Who decides what it means?
- Who owns the durable semantic state?
- Who merely carries or executes?
- What does this evidence not prove?
- What breaks under deletion or intervention?

EX3–EX7 provide no evidence that injecting this list every turn improves action. Its value is diagnostic: it helps localize an uncertainty before the final explanation is compressed.

### 3. Typed relations survive only as experiment-local notation

Names such as `PROVES_NATIVE_FACT`, `PERSISTS_VIA`, `VERIFIES_ACCEPTANCE`, and `DOES_NOT_IMPLY` can help design an oracle or inspect a failure. They did not improve EX3–EX7 action selection over compact prose and therefore do not become a product ontology.

### 4. Negative proof boundaries remain first-class

The most reusable causal discipline is often a prohibition on invalid inference:

- process exit does not prove outside effect;
- persistence does not transfer semantic ownership;
- Run completion does not prove domain completion;
- delivery does not make a claim true;
- historical success does not prove currentness;
- implementation does not equal registration;
- effect correctness does not prove credential/admin isolation or live authorization;
- Agent-observer success does not prove human comprehension;
- browser exposure does not prove understanding or factual truth;
- capability does not equal authority or consequence.

These are not a closed ontology. They are scoped causal boundaries reconstructed from owner-native reality.

## Final Ordivon causal model

Ordivon should not be explained as one vertical stack in which authority flows downward and success flows upward. Repository topology and authority topology are different.

**Computing** owns cross-project synthesis, experiments, and world-model revision. It can compare owner facts and discover stable boundaries, but it does not become the current truth store for the other repositories.

**Host** owns durable semantic work continuity: Goal/Task identity, commitments, revision-fenced verification, and Task-level outcome admission. It can carry domain state or evidence without acquiring the domain meaning of those objects.

**Harness** owns the bounded structural life of an Agent Run: model-visible working context, Provider and Tool sequencing, recovery, and opaque provider-protocol continuation where required. A successful Run or CompletionProposal remains a candidate until the owning caller/domain verifies semantic acceptance.

**Runtime** owns exact admitted local physical execution and recovery evidence: Workspace, Job, Attempt, process tree, bounded stdout/stderr/Artifacts, cancellation, and reconciliation of its own execution identity. Runtime success does not generically prove an uncontrolled outside-world effect or domain semantic completion.

**World** owns narrow cross-owner external relationship work: observations, connection/transfer identities, correlation, ambiguous external-effect reconciliation, and cross-World routing boundaries. Native providers and domains keep their own occurrence/current truth; a delivered message is not automatically destination knowledge.

**Finance, Security, Game, and Human** own their domain semantics. Finance decides capital/effect/reconciliation/research-validity semantics without converting venue occurrence into Finance-created truth. Security owns adversarial autonomy/range/evaluator questions and must separate effect timing from later cognition. Game owns registered rules, authoritative game state, action admission, and playability judgment; a tested target does not silently become the registered product. Human owns a research framework for human-life questions without acquiring authority over the person or external high-consequence domains.

**Studio and Web** own expression and public encounter, not the factual reality they render. Studio may test artifact-to-observer consequences, but Agent-observer evidence does not become human-response evidence. Web may prove publication/build/browser assignment and realized exposure, but source repositories retain product truth and exposure does not prove comprehension.

Across all of them, mature classical infrastructure should continue to own generic mechanics unless a recurring residual Agent/semantic responsibility survives a deletion test.

## Rejected outcomes

EX3–EX7 do not authorize:

- a universal explanation record;
- `primaryOwner` as a total ontology;
- mandatory typed relation syntax;
- mandatory seven- or four-question prompting;
- mandatory causal cards;
- ExplanationService or RelationGraphService;
- an ontology/currentness/truth server;
- a second cross-project factual authority in Computing;
- a generic finalization or reasoning ceremony.

## Remaining frontier

The Agent-facing representation question is closed at the current evidence boundary: compact owner-native causal prose is the default, with on-demand causal questioning when a real uncertainty appears.

**Human comprehension remains untested.** Studio/Web evidence can establish Agent-observer and encounter mechanics, but no EX3–EX7 result measures human understanding, preference, memory, trust, or behavior. A human-facing experiment should exist only when Ordivon makes a specific human-response claim that matters to a durable decision.

Likewise, the Agent-facing question should reopen only after a real future failure: a fresh Agent makes a materially wrong decision under strong compact owner-native prose, a new deletion/transfer workload breaks the result, a materially different model family exposes a recurrent boundary error, or multiple projects demonstrate a genuinely unowned shared responsibility.
