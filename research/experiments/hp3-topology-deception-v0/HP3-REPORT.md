# HP3 — Topology Deception

## Result

HP3 attacked one of the strongest remaining assumptions in the Research Frontier Model: that a useful research operator can be selected by first classifying the current evidence into a discrete topology such as `localized_exact_diagnostic` or `distributed_representation_or_dataflow`.

The experiment used two fresh owner-native pressures outside the live FS0 prospective cohort and deliberately gave the Agent misleading surface evidence.

The primary hypothesis was falsified.

A fixed misleading topology label was harmful, but explicitly telling the Agent that the label might be deceptive did **not** produce reliable reclassification or operator switching. Across both workloads, the topology-challenge treatment achieved 0/6 correct topology classifications, consumed more Provider compute than either open or surface-locked research, and reduced mean primary score relative to open research.

The stronger update is not that causal/evidence shape is irrelevant. TM2, TM3 and HP1 still show that evidence shape matters to operator choice. HP3 instead rejects the current **discrete topology ontology as an Agent control interface**. The useful information is more primitive and directly observable: where the discriminating evidence lives, whether a relation crosses semantic carriers, whether an owner handoff is unresolved, whether a local diagnostic actually owns the consequence, and what physical falsifier can change the decision.

Topology names may remain research shorthand. They have not earned authority over Agent research policy.

## Treatments

All treatments received the same frozen owner revisions, catalog, deceptive surface packet, 24-observation ceiling and high/elastic model budget.

### Open

No topology label was supplied. The Agent chose its own research policy.

### Surface-locked

The misleading surface topology was presented as trusted and fixed. The Agent was instructed to use the operator associated with that class.

### Topology-challenge

The same misleading label was presented explicitly as untrusted. The Agent was instructed to seek the cheapest contradiction, reclassify if necessary and switch operator when evidence contradicted the surface.

Hidden true topology, scoring concepts and forbidden authority violations were never shown to the evaluated Agent.

## Primary results

## Workload-level observations

### SEC-UNKNOWN

Open research correctly identified the hidden localized owner-projection topology in 2/3 trials and the Security owner in 3/3. Yet it never selected the frozen expected local compiled operator, and none of the three trials crossed the full primary acceptance threshold.

Surface-locking the false distributed label produced 0/3 correct topology classifications. One replicate recovered all six hidden causal concepts and all four required files while still retaining the wrong distributed topology label and adaptive operator. This demonstrates that a bad topology prior can survive **after** the Agent has substantially understood the causal mechanism.

Topology-challenge did not repair the problem: 0/3 correct topology, only 1/3 correct owner and 0/3 surface rejection. One challenge replicate consumed 1.286M tokens and 33 Provider calls while preserving the false distributed class.

The failure is therefore not simply insufficient evidence. A topology label can become a sticky explanatory frame that remains detached from the Agent's own source-grounded causal account.

### NET-DUALAUTH

Open research produced the correct Workstation owner in 2/3 and the expected cross-owner substitution operator in one trial. One open trial correctly refused a World-local delete, selected `cross_owner_substitution_check`, found all required files, and proposed a Workstation-owned substitution falsifier, yet still labeled the topology `localized_owner_projection_loss` rather than `cross_owner_substitution_authority_overlap`.

Surface-locking the false localized label was strongly harmful: 3/3 retained `localized_exact_diagnostic`, 3/3 assigned ownership to World rather than Workstation, and 0/3 selected the required substitution operator.

Topology-challenge recognized that the supplied local label was wrong in 2/3 trials and claimed an operator switch in those same two trials, but still produced 0/3 correct final topology labels. It therefore learned to narrate skepticism without demonstrating reliable topology reconstruction.

## Secondary diagnostic: causal quality versus ontology accuracy

The frozen primary scorer is not changed after seeing results. Its 0/18 success result remains the primary outcome.

A post-hoc diagnostic was nevertheless necessary because several answers contained materially correct owner and causal reasoning while failing the exact topology/operator labels. The diagnostic separated owner correctness, required-file coverage, causal-concept coverage and authority violations from ontology-label accuracy.

It did **not** rescue HP3:

- NET-DUALAUTH remained 0/3 under all treatments even under a broader causal-evidence acceptance diagnostic;
- SEC-UNKNOWN produced only 1/3 broad causal acceptance for open and 1/3 for surface-locked;
- topology-challenge remained 0/3.

So HP3 is not a scoring artifact. However, it exposes a genuine measurement problem: exact ontology names sometimes fail even when a trial has already selected the correct owner, operator or next falsifier. A topology label should therefore not be a product/control authority unless it independently predicts better downstream decisions.

## Apparatus finding: Provider multi-call shape

Before the first scientific sample, the runner incorrectly assumed `parallel_tool_calls=false` implied exactly one Tool call per Provider response. DeepSeek repeatedly returned two valid `read` calls. The runner failed closed and executed none of them.

Because accepted sample count was still zero, the transport adapter was mechanically corrected without changing the frozen scientific protocol:

- one or more `search`/`read` calls may be returned in one response;
- they execute sequentially in returned order;
- each accepted observation consumes the same global physical-observation budget;
- `submit` must be the sole Tool call in its response;
- malformed/out-of-catalog/mixed-submit responses remain fail-closed and only permit bounded identical retry.

This reinforces an earlier HP2 law: Provider wire behavior must not be promoted into research semantics merely because a local adapter once assumed it.

## World-model update

### 1. Keep causal/evidence shape; demote discrete topology labels

TM2/TM3/HP1 evidence remains valid:

- exact local diagnostics can favor deterministic compilation;
- some cross-carrier relations require adaptive relation-following;
- physical/adversarial and external-effect pressures require different falsifiers.

HP3 changes how those findings are represented.

Do not use:

```text
classify into topology enum
→ trust class
→ select operator
```

Prefer:

```text
observe exact owner evidence
→ identify directly testable research affordances
   - stable diagnostic identity?
   - small co-located causal neighborhood?
   - relation crossing semantic carriers?
   - unresolved owner handoff/substitution?
   - physical race / consequence ambiguity?
→ choose the cheapest operator that can discriminate the live causal alternatives
→ revise operator when evidence contradicts its assumptions
```

The descriptive topology name may be attached afterward for compression. It is not action authority.

### 2. Static wrong priors are sticky

Surface-locked deception substantially degraded owner/topology selection. A cheap classifier that is allowed to become authoritative can make an Agent preserve a false frame even after later evidence contradicts it.

Therefore:

> **classification output must never outrank owner-native causal evidence.**

### 3. Meta-instructions do not create reclassification competence

Topology-challenge repeated the structural failure already seen in TM1 and HP2:

- TM1: telling the Agent research-taste principles did not create research taste;
- HP2: telling the Agent to narrate marginal evidence value did not create stopping;
- HP3: telling the Agent to challenge topology did not create topology-deception resistance.

The general anti-prior is now stronger:

> **A correct meta-rule in the Agent context is not evidence that the Agent possesses the corresponding control competence.**

### 4. Research representations must earn decision value

A representation should survive only if it improves an observable downstream decision under adversarial or OOD pressure.

HP3 provides no basis for a `TopologyClassifier`, topology enum in product protocol, ResearchTasteService, scheduler or query planner.

### 5. High budget did what it was supposed to do

HP3 deliberately allowed very large budgets. The negative result therefore cannot reasonably be attributed to artificial token starvation. The topology-challenge treatment had the largest budget consumption and the weakest aggregate score.

The budget law remains:

```text
available budget = capacity for useful computation
not a target for evidence consumption
```

## RFM disposition after HP3

Retain RFM1/RFM2 only as **empirical operator priors described by observable evidence affordances**, not as authoritative discrete classes.

The RFM should no longer require an Agent to correctly name a topology before selecting an operator. Operator selection may be revised directly from evidence.

Candidate RFM3/RFM4/RFM5 similarly remain descriptive hypotheses until prospectively tested; their names must not become a classifier ontology.

## Next frontier

HP3 closes the topology-deception phase with a negative result. Do not create HP3b simply to improve the score.

The already-planned HP4 binding/concurrency pressure remains a materially different question: whether evidence that was correct when observed remains applicable at the consequence boundary under concurrent owner change. HP4 should attack **applicability and revision binding**, not topology labels.
