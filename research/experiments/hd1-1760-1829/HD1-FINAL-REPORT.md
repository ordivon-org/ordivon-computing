# HD1 Final Report — 1760–1829 Historical Theory Dogfood

## Executive result

HD1 is complete.

It reconstructed **40 source-locked innovation/discovery trajectories** from the first industrial-revolution era, including 12 deep Anchors, explicit failed routes, parallel discoveries, delayed ideas, standards/institutions, safety corrections and an under-recognized skills substrate. The frozen LW0 hypotheses remained byte-identical throughout source collection and theory coding.

The main result is not that 1760–1829 'proves Ordivon right'. The history is more discriminating:

1. several modern semantic distinctions recur strongly outside computing;
2. several Agent-era hypotheses become much narrower or simply non-applicable;
3. a fresh independent coder exposes substantial codebook elasticity, so retrospective SUPPORTS counts are not a trustworthy convergence score;
4. de-identified historical replay shows strong research-control behavior on three of four cases and a clean miss on the fourth;
5. history surfaces several recurring residual structures—especially complementarity, diffusion latency, instrumentation, standards, distributed invention and scale-induced boundary shifts—that are not allowed to become new priors until later epochs independently reproduce them.

## Pass A — source-native historical reconstruction

HD1 expanded to 105 candidates before contracting to the exact 40-case roster. Every frozen trajectory now has a source-native evidence bundle; all 12 Deep Anchors satisfy the source contract with explicit lineage boundaries.

The strongest source-native observations are:

- **validation is not one transition**: proof of mechanism, repeatability, manufacturability, institutional admission and broad diffusion often occur at different times;
- **the useful unit is usually a system trajectory rather than an inventor event**;
- **new observation/instrument capability can outlive or exceed the theory that motivated it**;
- **a productive scientific model can contain wrong components**;
- **technical definitions and standards require physical/institutional realization**;
- **organizational and material complements frequently dominate whether a technical capability produces economic consequence**;
- **local success does not imply general diffusion**;
- **scale can change the objective and reveal new harms or affected parties**.

The new Anchors A09–A12 sharpen these points:

### A09 — geological mapping

William Smith's fossil-succession method enabled national-scale stratigraphic correlation, but Greenough's later distributed-observation map was more accurate in some regions because Smith sometimes extrapolated strata from his unifying model beyond direct evidence. Strong model coherence and broad observation coverage were both valuable; neither dominated unconditionally.

### A10 — railway system

Trevithick's high-pressure locomotive experiments demonstrated useful rail adhesion while exposing track strength as a new bottleneck. Public railway operation and then Rainhill turned the problem into a coupled system of engine, rails, operations and evaluation. Rainhill judged more than speed; reliability, fuel, load and construction all mattered, and the trial procedure itself had to be clarified as the experiment ran.

### A11 — Leblanc alkali

The chemical process could work while the first commercialization failed under war, diverted inputs, confiscation and institutional discontinuity. Later industrial success then created severe pollution. Chemical validity, commercial tractability and broader social consequence are separate axes.

### A12 — cotton gin / Atlantic cotton system

A major local processing bottleneck was reduced, yet the larger system moved in the opposite direction from a naive labor-saving story: profitable cotton expansion increased demand for enslaved field labor. Patent success, inventor profit, local throughput and system-level human consequence are distinct truths.

### The largest historical narrowing: S3

HD1 does **not** support a simplistic reading that a genuine improvement must win in arbitrarily different worlds.

Instead:

> an improvement must survive tests that are materially different **inside the claim's declared applicability scope**.

Water power beating steam at one municipal site does not make the steam engine unreal; Portsmouth's special-purpose line remaining local does not make it a failure; a correct scientific representation can remain undeployable until missing complements arrive. Generalization evidence must bind the claim being made.

### S5 is not universalized

`Persistent State ≠ raw transcript` is central in modern Agent systems, but 35 of 40 HD1 trajectories are simply not instances of that problem. Maps, nomenclature, standards and punch-card programs provide partial analogues, yet history gives no license to turn S5 into a universal innovation law.

### F4 has zero direct HD1 evidence

The Agent-era anti-law `raw accumulated context / arbitrary lossy compaction = sufficient state` receives no direct first-industrial-revolution evidence. It remains useful where its phenomenon exists; historical OOD does not promote it.

## Confirmation-bias / codebook audit

A fresh DeepSeek context was given only:

- the exact frozen S1–S10 definitions;
- the 12 source-locked Deep Anchor histories;
- a strong instruction to prefer `NOT_APPLICABLE` over metaphorical analogy;
- no access to the primary Pass B verdicts.

The result is a significant measurement warning:


The independent coder was **not** simply more skeptical. It often judged historical analogies as `SUPPORTS` more aggressively—for example all 12 Deep Anchors were mapped to S7 metaproductivity—while the primary coder deliberately left five S7 cases N/A or NARROWS.

Therefore the problem is not 'primary coder too positive'. It is that several high-level labels, especially S6 and the SUPPORTS/NARROWS boundary of S3, are semantically elastic.

HD1 consequently rejects using raw coding frequency as a universal convergence score.

The stronger evidence ordering becomes:

```text
source-native historical facts / failures
> prospective prediction error
> residual recurrence across independent epochs
> stable coarse applicability across coders
> exact retrospective SUPPORTS/NARROWS counts
```

This is an important theoretical result:

> stable structures in Reality can exist even when our current taxonomy for describing them is unstable.

`Classifier convergence ≠ Reality convergence`.

## Historical prospective replay

Four de-identified cases were run with three independent `deepseek-v4-flash` submissions each. Names, exact dates, locations and famous product labels were removed where possible; hidden outcomes were not in the evaluated context.

Total live experiment:

```text
12 Provider calls
20,725 total tokens
0 transport retries
58 / 60 epistemic-control rubric points
9 / 12 route-direction hits
12 / 12 explicit generalization boundaries
```

The rubric scores bottleneck identification, discriminating-test quality, preservation of serious rivals, complement awareness and generalization boundary separately from whether the historically winning route was ranked first.

### HR-A05 — electrical controversy

3/3 bets selected the animal-free repeated conductor/electrolyte stack as the discriminating intervention and explicitly refused to infer that success would prove all animal electricity nonexistent. This closely matches the historical pile→electrolysis transition while preserving the later-valid bioelectric line.

### HR-A08 — longitude

3/3 rejected 'one successful voyage is enough'. All demanded independent reproduction and realistic repeated testing; the bets preserved the astronomical solution family rather than treating a timekeeper pass as total victory. This closely matches the historical Board reproduction requirement and long method overlap.

### HR-A09 — geological mapping

3/3 selected model-guided mapping plus independent regional checks and retained distributed observation as a serious route. This closely matches the later correction: the unifying fossil-stratigraphy model was powerful, while distributed observations corrected some over-extrapolation.

### HR-A03 — precision manufacturing: the useful miss

All three bets identified precision, throughput, measurement and transferability correctly. But all preferred first investing in a **more general-purpose machine-tool pilot** and deferred the specialized sequential production line.

The hidden local history goes the other way: concentrated repeated naval demand made the specialized Portsmouth system work exceptionally well for its intended job, even though the more general precision-machine principles proved more reusable elsewhere.

This is the cleanest HD1 research-taste falsifier:

> **generality / metaproductivity is not automatically the best next ResearchBet.**

A specialized route can dominate under concentrated demand and a narrow, stable world even if another route has greater long-run transfer value.

This is directly relevant to future Ordivon Frontier Selection: `future optionality` is one value dimension, not a trump card over closability, local objective and current world structure.

### Replay validity boundary

These are calibration experiments, not clean estimates of counterfactual eighteenth-century research skill. A pretrained model may recognize structural patterns from historical training data even after de-identification. The experiment shows that the packet can elicit useful research-control behavior and prediction error; it does not prove independent rediscovery.

## What HD1 says about the convergence hypothesis

The original strong intuition—'correct things converge, errors are chaos'—survives only in a refined form.

HD1 strongly supports that many famous historical outcomes are causally compressed stories: when reconstructed, they repeatedly contain validation boundaries, serious rivals, complements, diffusion lags and institutional realities. But the 55% exact coding agreement warns that **our chosen labels are not yet the stable structure itself**.

The current claim should therefore remain:

> independent worlds appear to repeatedly force a smaller set of structural pressures, while both successful implementations and failure manifestations vary widely; our taxonomy of those structures must itself be experimentally calibrated.

This is deliberately weaker than claiming a universal historical law.

## RSI implications

HD1 changes the Research Frontier world model in five ways without changing the frozen HD0 theory file:

1. **Applicability before generality** — ask what exact world/scope a claim is supposed to survive, not whether it wins everywhere.
2. **Generality is a value dimension, not a default selector** — A03 replay is a direct counterexample.
3. **Evaluator and taxonomy uncertainty are first-class** — coding disagreement is evidence about the measurement instrument, not noise to hide.
4. **Metaproductivity is morally/value incomplete** — a system can increase future production capability while increasing catastrophic harm.
5. **Historical residual recurrence may be more informative than forcing old labels** — complements, diffusion, instrumentation and standards should remain visible until later epochs decide whether they recur.

## Instrument failures retained as evidence

Two experiment-apparatus errors occurred and were preserved:

1. an early candidate-count regex was wrong and produced the absurd value `13883`; direct line parsing corrected the real count to 105;
2. the first independent-coder runner compared lexicographically sorted strings to natural `S1..S10` order, so valid output containing all ten IDs was rejected because `S10` sorts before `S2`. That run was excluded; the check was changed only to exact set equivalence and the full experiment rerun.

Neither failure changed historical evidence, frozen hypotheses or model prompts. They are useful reminders that experiment infrastructure is itself fallible.
