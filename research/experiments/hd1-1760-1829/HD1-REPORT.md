## Candidate expansion

The 105-case pool includes famous inventions, failed routes, serious rivals, standards, institutions, global technology-transfer systems, delayed discoveries, safety corrections and low-glamour complements. It is intentionally redundant at the precursor/rival level so the contraction pass can decide which items belong inside one causal trajectory.

This changed the historical ontology in practice. A simple famous-invention list would count Watt, the condenser, rotary steam, Wilkinson boring and Boulton separately; HD1 instead treats them as interacting parts of a prime-mover trajectory where appropriate. Conversely, high-pressure mobile steam and the railway system remain separate from Watt because they introduce materially different pressure, authority, safety, infrastructure and diffusion conditions.

## First source-native batch: A01–A08

### A01 — Watt / factory steam

The source bundle does not support a single-event 'Watt invented the steam engine' story. The pressure was a specific thermal/economic defect in an existing atmospheric-engine world. The separate condenser addressed that defect, but industrial consequence depended on financing, Boulton's organization, accurate cylinders, rotary mechanisms, on-site construction and customer economics.

### A02 — cotton spinning

No single spinning machine dominates the causal reconstruction. Hargreaves multiplied hand-powered spindles; Arkwright coupled roller drafting to water power and mills; Crompton combined principles to obtain fine yarn. Later plant-level evidence shows that mechanized spinning also required organizational learning: adoption could precede high productivity by years.

### A03 — precision machine tools

This Anchor separates two consequences often collapsed in textbook history. Wilkinson/Maudslay encoded accuracy into machine geometry and generated broadly reusable precision capability. Portsmouth's integrated block-making system was technically successful and extremely durable, but remained unusually local rather than immediately becoming a general British production pattern. Local success and general diffusion are therefore separate historical facts before any LW0 coding.

### A04 — Lavoisier

The source bundle supports quantitative weighing, reaction accounting, oxygen chemistry, water composition and a new chemical language. Independent history-of-science literature also disputes the clean 'total revolution' story: important ontology/method continuity and Lavoisier's own caloric framework survived. HD1 therefore locks both the achievement and its historical boundary before theory coding.

### A05 — Galvani / Volta / electrochemistry

A causal dispute generated a new experimental instrument. Volta's pile removed animal tissue from one key current-generation demonstration and made continuous current available; Nicholson/Carlisle and Davy then used that capability to open electrochemical decomposition. Later electrophysiology also vindicated important parts of Galvani's animal-electricity insight. The durable capability outran the initial binary controversy.

### A06 — metric metrology

The trajectory separates definition from realization. A natural/geodetic aspiration still had to be materialized in 1799 platinum reference artifacts and embedded in institutions/calibration chains. The source bundle is sufficient for that definition→artifact→institution story, but not yet for detailed political-adoption claims; those require French primary commission/legal records if they later become decisive.

### A07 — vaccination

Jenner sits inside a much older and geographically distributed immunity/variolation history. His importance lies partly in deliberate cowpox→smallpox challenge evidence and public documentation, not in inventing the observation that exposure could protect. Independent physicians rapidly scrutinized the claim, while scaling required a living biological-material logistics network long before modern immunology explained the mechanism.

### A08 — longitude

This is the strongest first-batch example of evaluation plurality. H4 performed well, yet the Board did not treat one voyage as sufficient. Its 1765 proposal required disclosure and other makers to reproduce adequate timekeepers before the full reward. At the same time lunar-distance navigation matured through the Nautical Almanac. The historical outcome is therefore not 'clock beats astronomy' but a long period of competing methods, verification, manufacturability and institutional judgment.

## Source-contract audit

All eight Anchors now meet the HD0 deep-source contract with explicit boundaries. The audit refuses to count URLs as independent evidence lineages.

Examples:

- two digital copies of Lavoisier's 1789 `Traité` remain one primary-work lineage;
- multiple Science Museum object pages are one institutional lineage unless the physical artifacts themselves are materially independent evidence;
- H4 and Board records are strong primary evidence but much of the archive remains in one Greenwich/Board family, so normative claims about the Board–Harrison dispute need broader historiography;
- the metric Anchor has material 1799 primary objects but not yet a French primary political/legal dossier.

The trajectory schema itself survives the first physical dogfood. No schema expansion is justified yet.

## First methodological lessons — still pre-theory

These are source-native observations, **not LW0 verdicts**:

1. famous 'inventions' often become systems when reconstructed causally;
2. technical validation, repeatability, manufacturability and diffusion are distinct transitions;
3. an instrument can become more historically important than the theory that originally motivated it;
4. correct/useful results can coexist with wrong surrounding theory;
5. standards require physical/institutional realization, not only elegant definitions;
6. organization and skilled complements frequently determine whether a machine becomes productive;
7. one successful trial is repeatedly insufficient for system-scale consequence;
8. locally successful mechanisms can remain historically non-general for decades.

The next pass is allowed to ask whether these locked histories support, narrow or contradict S1–S10/F1–F7. It is not allowed to rewrite those frozen hypotheses.
