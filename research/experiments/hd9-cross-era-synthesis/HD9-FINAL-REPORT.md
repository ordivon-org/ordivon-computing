## 4. Cross-Era Structural Map v0

The strongest synthesis permitted by the evidence has seven typed families, explicitly **not exhaustive universal laws**:

1. **Complementary capability formation** — capability is often co-produced by components, infrastructure, skills, process and institutions; resolving one bottleneck can move the governing bottleneck.
2. **Staged realization and validation** — discovery, validation, production, deployment, diffusion, durability and evaluator standing do not collapse into one status.
3. **Observability expands the reachable question space** — instruments and evidence infrastructure change which hypotheses are discriminable and how costly future search is.
4. **Representations and standards as coordination substrates** — representation is not reality, yet shared representations/interfaces can causally organize distributed production.
5. **Scale-induced boundary dynamics** — scale can move bottlenecks, expose new causal/value dimensions and generate externalities, thereby changing the relevant problem itself.
6. **Distributed causation and historical attribution** — hero-inventor/date narratives often compress multi-actor, multi-stage causal contribution.
7. **Objective-relative frontier choice** — local value, generality, future option value, metaproductivity and time-to-effect are distinct axes; there is no universal greatness score.

These seven families are better thought of as a **research-dynamics map** than a theory claiming that every invention follows the same pathway.

## 7. Research taste after 266 years of history

The historical programme strengthens the **world model behind research taste**, not static “research taste prose.” TM1 already showed that giving an Agent correct maxims does not create research-taste competence.

History instead adds conditional structure to the research problem:

- improving observability can change what can even be asked;
- complements determine whether a validated principle becomes usable capability;
- different realization stages require different evidence and evaluators;
- standards can alter coordination topology;
- scale can generate a new problem rather than merely a larger version of the old one;
- generality, metaproductivity and local utility must be judged against objective and horizon.

So the useful object is not “a list of genius rules,” but a revisable model of **how questions, evidence, capability, realization and consequences transform one another**.
