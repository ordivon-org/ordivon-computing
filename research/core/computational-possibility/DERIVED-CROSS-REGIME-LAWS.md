# Derived Cross-Regime Laws

Canonical derived structures include:

- claim formation over already constituted computational situations; `Underconstituted != Impossible`;
- guarded regime-relative achievability/existence notation with explicit/recoverable quantifier, role and dependency structure;
- impossibility as negative achievability under the same constituted typed inputs, with `NoWitnessFound != NoWitnessExists`;
- joint/contextual admissibility controls for composition; componentwise witness existence does not imply composite achievability;
- complexity, bounds, and Pareto/frontier structure as constraint-indexed achievability;
- typed reductions, simulations, translations and capability lifts with explicit scope/coverage, parametricity and semantic/computational preservation obligations;
- capability interaction contracts for higher-order substitution, including provider choice/history/law semantics and assumption/guarantee refinement only where theorem-bearing;
- directional theorem variance: witness-preserving transport propagates achievability forward and impossibility backward; quantitative upper/lower transfer additionally requires domination/reflection structure;
- qualified presentation invariance rather than absolute representation invariance;
- local contract-demand refinement, provider guarantee/refinement, client robustness, regime power, reduction/hardness and resource preorders as distinct order-like structures;
- comparative computational power over an explicit obligation universe;
- induced hardness, completeness, degree, and order structures;
- uniform/nonuniform, advice, preprocessing/training, dynamic/amortized, sequential, relational, stateful, stochastic and meta-computational variants as typed regime/index/witness/interaction constructions.

The detailed derived transport/capability laws are materialized in `DERIVED-TRANSPORT-CAPABILITY-CALCULUS.md`.

Useful notation such as `CExists_R(Ω;Γ)` remains derived shorthand. Utility does not make it a numbered Foundation.
