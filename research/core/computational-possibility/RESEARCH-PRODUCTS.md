# Computational Possibility — Research Products and Consumer Boundaries

## 1. Purpose

Computational Possibility (CP) is not only a repository of computability/complexity claims. Its reusable owner-level output is **scope-preserving computational inference**: constitute the computational question, analyze the resulting possibility structure, compare/transport claims across situations or capabilities, and control what a formal result licenses about an actual external target.

This document is an explanatory recovery/consumer surface. It does **not** introduce a Foundation, a ninth situation axis, a mandatory workflow, a universal record schema, a service, or an authority registry.

The canonical theory remains in `CORE-THEORY.md` and `DERIVED-TRANSPORT-CAPABILITY-CALCULUS.md`. Actual external facts remain with their owners.

## 2. Theory-level picture

A useful non-canonical explanatory picture is:

`Constituted Computational Situation -> Local Possibility Structure -> Typed Relations/Transport -> Actual Applicability`.

The levels are distinct:

- **Computational Situation** is the local claim/typing unit: what problem, information, interaction, quantifier and resource conditions are being asked about.
- **Admissible witness / agency / capability** is the candidate action unit relative to that situation.
- **Possibility structure** is the theory-level object of analysis: successful witness/capability structure, feasible regions/frontiers where relevant, and typed relations to other situations/capabilities.
- `Achievable(C)` is only the non-emptiness projection of the local successful-witness structure.

CP therefore studies how constituted computational conditions induce possibility structures and how those structures vary, compose, refine and transport.

## 3. Four first-class research-product families

### 3.1 Situation Analysis

**Question:** What exactly is the computational claim?

Situation Analysis freezes or diagnoses the load-bearing computational scope before a possibility judgment is made. It may expose:

- imported source/SCD meaning and representation references;
- obligation and promise/domain scope;
- role and quantifier order;
- information/revelation/visibility assumptions;
- witness/capability grammar;
- interaction, history, causal or adversarial structure;
- resource/performance constraints;
- external premises that are not CP-owned.

Its key negative outcome is `UNDERCONSTITUTED`, not `Impossible`.

Useful control questions include:

- Is this an instance claim, family claim, uniform claim or contextual claim?
- Who controls each choice: client, provider, adversary or stochastic law?
- What information is available when each decision is made?
- What exact promise, threshold, resource and aggregation semantics are intended?

A future Situation/Claim Card may be useful as a thin recovery projection, but no fixed eight-field form or duplicated Research-System claim authority is currently required.

### 3.2 Local Possibility Analysis

**Question:** What is computationally possible in this constituted situation?

For fixed `C`, Local Possibility Analysis studies the theorem-appropriate local possibility structure, including:

- admitted and successful witness/strategy/capability families;
- `Achievable(C)` / `Impossible(C)` at exact scope;
- resource-constrained feasible regions and Pareto/frontier structure;
- approximation, probability, uniformity or interaction-sensitive feasibility;
- the exact scope of lower-bound or impossibility claims.

Complexity/resource analysis is a specialization of this family: vary the declared `Phi` constraint/profile and study the changing feasible region/frontier.

Impossibility guards also belong here. Permanent distinctions include:

- `Underconstituted != Impossible`;
- `NoWitnessFound != NoWitnessExists`;
- `ProofMethodBarrier != ComputationalLowerBound`;
- `ProxyModelBarrier != TargetRegimeComputationalBarrier`.

### 3.3 Comparative / Transport Analysis

**Question:** What changes, refines, substitutes, reduces or transports when the computational situation/capability changes?

This is CP's main cross-situation product family. It includes:

- contract-demand refinement;
- regime-power comparison;
- reduction, simulation and hardness relations;
- capability/provider substitution;
- composition and middle-contract compatibility;
- qualified presentation/representation transport;
- qualitative, contextual and quantitative theorem transfer.

A comparison must say **which relation/order is intended**. CP keeps distinct:

- contract demand;
- provider guarantee/refinement;
- client robustness;
- regime computational power;
- reduction/hardness;
- resource/performance preorder/frontier.

Unqualified statements such as `A is stronger than B` are under-specified when more than one of these orders could be meant.

Capability substitution is consumer-relative. Shared API shape, route admission or one successful fixture is not global capability equivalence. The full required capability interaction contract determines which assumptions/guarantees must be preserved.

A future Transport/Refinement Record may be useful when repeated consumers need a durable summary of required contract, produced contract, preserved burdens, missing burdens and owner-premise references. No generic transport registry or graph is currently earned.

### 3.4 Applicability Analysis

**Question:** What does a formal CP result actually license us to say about this external target now?

Applicability is the formal-to-actual boundary already materialized in `APPLICABILITY-SUBSYSTEM.md`.

The semantic blocks are:

- `ClaimScope`;
- `TargetScope`;
- `TransportBurden`;
- `PremiseEvidence`;
- `Verdict`.

Constructive/upper claims require a formal-to-actual implementation/embedding burden. Impossibility/lower claims require the stronger opposite-direction coverage/reflection burden from relevant actual candidates into the formal model.

Verdicts remain bounded:

- `APPLIES`;
- `DOES_NOT_APPLY`;
- `UNRESOLVED`;
- `EXPIRED`.

Applicability never copies Runtime, Interlocus, Harness, World, Security or source-domain truth into timeless CP authority.

## 4. Consumer boundary map

| Consumer / owner | What CP may provide | What CP does not own |
|---|---|---|
| SCD / source semantics | computational preservation burden beyond semantic equivalence/refinement | meaning, representation truth, semantic equivalence/refinement itself |
| Harness | formal capability substitution/composition analysis over declared tool/provider contracts | actual Run admission, provider construction, tool provisioning, orchestration |
| Runtime | scope discipline between execution evidence and quantified computational claims; formal implementation/resource transport burdens | actual Job/Attempt/process/resource/execution truth |
| Interlocus | abstract communication-constrained feasibility and regime comparison; applicability of formal communication assumptions | actual route/topology/reachability/service capability |
| World | formal realizability claims conditioned on declared physical/substrate assumptions | physical actuality/realizability facts |
| Normative | whether a source-owned normative constraint leaves a computationally realizable witness/strategy space | permission, obligation, legitimacy or normative consequence truth |
| Game / Mechanism / Finance / other source owners | computational feasibility of source-defined strategies, solutions or obligations | payoff, market, equilibrium, value or domain semantics |

The general seam is:

`External owner constitutes meaning/actual premise -> CP analyzes computational possibility/transport -> external owner retains truth about actual realization/use.`

`Reference != Ownership`.

## 5. Dogfood evidence for the consumer contract

### Harness provider-route case

Current Harness Campaign-5 evidence established bounded co-admission of two distinct provider/adapter/model routes under the same exact `HarnessProviderUsePolicy U`, while route/input/policy drift fails closed.

CP consumption exposed three different claims that must not collapse:

1. policy admission under `U`;
2. workload-level computational capability/substitutability;
3. current live-provider applicability.

The first has bounded direct Harness evidence. The second requires the full consumer workload/capability contract. The third requires current external-owner evidence.

Permanent practical controls:

- `SameUsePolicyAdmission != CapabilitySubstitutability`;
- `SameRunStopCode != SameComputationalCapability`;
- `ControlledTransportSuccess != LiveProviderEquivalence`;
- `EndpointCoAdmissibility != DirectionalTransitionPreservation`.

This demonstrates a clean owner seam: Harness decides whether the concrete composition is admitted; CP analyzes whether the admitted replacement preserves the computational obligations the consumer actually requires.

### Runtime / Interlocus applicability case

A live 2026-08-19 observation found one scoped egress anchor/profile unavailable/non-listening while a separate pure local Runtime witness executed successfully.

CP consumption produced three different scoped consequences:

1. an exact local constructive witness can support a narrow `APPLIES` verdict;
2. a formal constructive claim requiring the unavailable exact egress capability cannot currently receive `APPLIES`, and an older current-capability basis may become `EXPIRED`;
3. the broader claim that the machine has no possible external path remains `UNRESOLVED` without exhaustive actual-candidate coverage.

Permanent practical controls:

- `HistoricalCapability != CurrentCapability`;
- `ExactRouteUnavailable != GlobalNetworkImpossibility`;
- `OneRouteFailure != ActualCandidateCoverage`.

This directly validates the asymmetric applicability law: one actual witness may establish a narrow constructive existence claim, while actual impossibility requires coverage of every relevant actual candidate class.

## 6. What CP produces in practice

The four product families support recurring decisions such as:

- Is the computational claim well-formed enough to evaluate?
- What is achievable under the declared information/interaction/resource contract?
- Which change in regime, resource or capability moves the possibility frontier?
- Can one provider/tool/capability safely substitute for another for this consumer contract?
- Does a reduction, hardness result, upper/lower bound or equivalence theorem transport to this target scope?
- Does a formal claim apply to the actual current system, or is the evidence mismatched, unresolved or stale?

The value is therefore not only theorem production. CP acts as a **computational inference firewall** against scope inflation across owners.

## 7. What CP should not become

Current evidence does not justify turning CP into:

- a universal algorithm ontology;
- a capability registry/database;
- a provider/tool taxonomy;
- a Runtime or Harness replacement;
- a network/current-state cache;
- an automatic theorem prover;
- an automatic owner-premise refresher;
- a generic workflow bureaucracy;
- a single scalar hardness/strength system;
- a category-theoretic universal transport engine.

Engineering materialization should remain demand-driven and thin.

## 8. Current project identity

A compact current characterization is:

> **Computational Possibility studies the possibility structures induced by constituted computational situations: their admissible forms of computational agency/capability, successful witness structures and resource frontiers, together with the typed relations through which possibility claims are refined, composed, substituted, compared, transported and applied.**

Its independent owner niche is:

> **scope-preserving inference from constituted computational conditions to formal possibility, comparative/transport consequences, and bounded actual applicability.**

This identity is consistent with the current zero-Foundation standing: the project's theoretical richness lives primarily in typing, quantification, admissibility, interaction, relations, composition and transport rather than a large primitive ontology.
