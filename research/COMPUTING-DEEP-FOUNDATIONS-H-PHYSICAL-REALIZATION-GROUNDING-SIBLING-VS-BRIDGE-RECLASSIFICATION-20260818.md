# 2. Primary rival grounding theories

The physical-computation literature itself does not yield one uncontested internal primitive.
Two strong rival families are especially useful.

## H-R1 — Abstraction / Representation Theory

Horsman, Stepney, Wagner and Kendon distinguish physical and abstract domains and use representation relations to state when physical evolution participates in computation.
The later ART treatment makes the relation between device and program explicit through a non-logical representation relation and commuting-diagram conditions, and explicitly relates the framework to refinement theory.

This pressure supports:

```text
PhysicalDynamicsAlone
!= Computation

AbstractProgramAlone
!= PhysicalComputation

ArbitraryStateMapping
!= ValidRealizationByIdentity
```

but also strongly suggests that the core relation is a bridge between domains rather than a third self-contained domain.

## H-R2 — Functional / Mechanistic account

Piccinini's mechanistic account treats concrete computing systems as functional mechanisms organized to manipulate medium-independent vehicles according to rules.
This supplies an independent anti-triviality criterion:

```text
AnyObserverChosenMapping
!= ComputingMechanism
```

because functional organization and rule-governed vehicle manipulation constrain the physical system independently of arbitrary interpretation.

But mechanistic grounding places major truthmakers in physical/functional organization rather than in a new abstract Computing substance.

## H-R3 — Independent H sibling

Prediction:

```text
After C/A/B/D/F/I + World/Hardware/Runtime subtraction,
there remains a self-contained Computing-owned realization responsibility
whose fields cannot be represented as a bridge among those owners.
```

## H-R4 — Typed cross-owner grounding bridge

Prediction:

```text
The physical↔computational realization relation remains nontrivial,
but its relata and validation conditions are distributed across existing owners.
The relation is therefore irreducible as a bridge/hyperedge,
not as a sibling node.
```

This pass attempts to destroy both H-R3 and H-R4; only one should survive.

```text

human-mediated open loops
```

```text

```

necessary by identity.

Therefore H cannot be rescued as a sibling merely by choosing one conventional computer ontology.

# 19. Why bridge is the correct architecture type

## 19.1 It is defined by heterogeneous relata

A sibling like B can state resource/feasibility claims entirely within an abstract computational model, though it may reference external resources.

H's core assertion is different:

```text
physical reality P realizes abstract computational structure C
```

Its very identity depends on one physical and one computational endpoint.

## 19.2 Removing either endpoint destroys the claim

```text
C without World/Hardware
→ only abstract semantics

World/Hardware without C
→ only physical dynamics/mechanism
```

The bridge exists only in their relation.

## 19.3 Alternative grounding theories change admissibility, not endpoint ownership

ART and mechanistic accounts disagree about what makes grounding non-arbitrary.
That disagreement belongs in `GroundingAdmissibilityBasis`, not in a new universal sibling essence.

## 19.4 Bridge failure explains miscomputation

A device can be physically real and computationally intended yet fail the declared bridge relation for some admitted state/operation.
This models miscomputation without requiring a sibling H substance.

Therefore:

```text
HBridgeIrreducibility
= ESTABLISHED AT CURRENT FRONTIER

HSiblingIrreducibility
= REJECTED
```
