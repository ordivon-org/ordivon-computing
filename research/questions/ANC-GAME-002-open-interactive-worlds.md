# ANC-GAME-002 — Open Interactive Worlds for People and Agents

> **Status:** deferred at M1. The thesis remains valid, but it does not authorize a generic social, habitat, or world platform; reactivation depends on the bounded post-alpha play experiment recorded in `portfolio.json`.

## Question

Which minimum world and experience structures allow persistent Agents to create intrinsic play, relationships, creation, emergence, habitation, and return motivation beyond task completion—without turning Ordivon Game into a general engine, social-simulation platform, Security-only range, or research-only laboratory?

## Why this question exists

`ANC-GAME-001` correctly resolved the boundary between Game-owned World semantics and one reusable Ordivon Host. It was intentionally framed around architecture, conformance, replay, coordination, and migration.

That framing is necessary but insufficient for the product's full purpose. Game can be misclassified as a deterministic laboratory whose value is always external to itself:

- evaluating models or Harnesses;
- generating Security trajectories;
- testing multi-Agent coordination;
- validating Host and Protocol contracts.

This question materially refines `ANC-GAME-001` after its convergence work closed. It asks what makes an Agent-native world worth entering as a game, playground, creative medium, social space, or persistent habitat.

## Project position

Ordivon Game may legitimately create:

```text
playgrounds and gardens
complete authored games
Agent-native companions and characters
creative and performance worlds
social and organizational worlds
persistent virtual habitats
research laboratories
Security adversarial ranges
```

Research and Security are important consumers, not universal product authorities.

Play, beauty, curiosity, mastery, surprise, attachment, expression, ritual, and shared history may be terminal values. They do not need to prove benchmark or productivity improvement.

## Baselines

### Classical authored game systems

Mature game engines, scripted quests, finite-state machines, behavior trees, utility systems, GOAP, systemic simulation, procedural generation, and multiplayer infrastructure already provide strong lower mechanisms.

Ordivon should reuse them when a real vertical needs them. This question does not authorize rebuilding a renderer, physics engine, ECS, pathfinder, networking stack, asset pipeline, or generic content platform.

### Social Agent sandboxes

*Generative Agents* and AI Town demonstrate memory, reflection, planning, and persistent social interaction. They establish possibility, not sufficient evidence of game quality, social validity, or general architecture.

### Open-ended embodied Agents

*Voyager* demonstrates exploration and compositional executable skill acquisition in Minecraft. XLand demonstrates open-ended multi-Agent task distributions and emergent behavior. SIMA demonstrates language-guided action across 3D games, and SIMA 2 extends the public direction toward reasoning and companion-like interaction.

These systems inform candidate mechanisms. They do not replace product judgment or prove that every mechanism belongs in Ordivon Game.

### Generated and large-scale worlds

Genie work and Project Sid expand the possible scale of generated environments and Agent populations. They also sharpen the need to separate plausibility from World truth, many messages from society, and generated volume from coherent experience.

## Current hypotheses

### H1 — Consequential openness creates more value than decorative fluency

An Agent contributes meaningfully when its language, memory, planning, relationship, or creation changes later World state, options, interpretation, or interaction. More dialogue without later consequence is not sufficient.

### H2 — Experience has an independent objective

Game evaluation must include play, return, surprise, attachment, expression, coherence, and player understanding. Mission success, reward, task completion, and model validity are not complete proxies.

### H3 — Persistent history must alter possibility

Memory and history earn retention when they affect future action, relationship, access, interpretation, or World state. Transcript accumulation without consequence should be compressed or deleted.

### H4 — A shared World Kernel can support plural verticals

Station Zero, a playground, a social world, a creative world, and a Security range may share authoritative state, observation, Action admission, replay, and recovery while retaining different content and experience semantics.

### H5 — A second world is required before generalization

Station Zero cannot justify a universal relationship model, society layer, construction protocol, narrative engine, or Agent habitat abstraction. A materially different second complete world must reproduce the same responsibility.

### H6 — Thin infrastructure enables broad reversible creativity

Core A-series minimalism should reduce permanent accidental structure while allowing abundant content and experimentation inside versioned, recoverable worlds.

## Minimum workload

The first post-alpha workload is `G-PLAY-001`:

- add one optional non-mission activity to Station Zero or a forked Scenario Case;
- require at least one Agent interpretation or Proposal;
- admit one player-visible persistent consequence;
- make the consequence alter a later relationship, observation, option, or World fact;
- preserve replay and process-replacement recovery;
- compare against a deterministic authored baseline;
- add no generic social, memory, scheduler, or civilization layer.

Candidate content includes a shared restoration project, a celebration, a relationship-changing disagreement and reconciliation, a non-critical created Artifact, or an Agent-proposed improvement. Product judgment selects the experience.

## Comparison

Run at least three cells:

```text
A. authored scripted activity
B. Agent language without persistent consequence
C. Agent participation with one admitted persistent consequence
```

Keep the World, presentation, and content budget as comparable as practical.

## Evidence

The first experiment should retain:

- exact Scenario Case, Provider, Context, Action, and consequence identity;
- replay and recovery equality;
- latency and inference cost;
- invalid, repetitive, incoherent, or context-breaking behavior;
- whether participants voluntarily engage with the optional activity;
- whether they can explain what changed and why it mattered;
- perceived continuity of character and relationship;
- evidence of return, replay, curiosity, attachment, surprise, or expression;
- a bounded qualitative review rather than inflated population claims.

## First falsifier

The hypothesis weakens if one small non-mission Agent activity fails to produce greater return, surprise, attachment, expression, consequential history, or meaningful choice than a scripted baseline at comparable total cost.

Other falsifiers include:

- persistent memory is not visible in later behavior;
- Agent output increases text but not experience;
- open behavior destroys World or aesthetic coherence;
- the player ignores optional activity because no meaningful affordance exists;
- model replacement destroys character continuity;
- research instrumentation dominates the player surface;
- every candidate mechanism remains unique to Station Zero.

## Valid outcomes

This question may conclude that Ordivon should:

- retain only authored non-mission content;
- use Agents for selected high-value characters rather than all NPCs;
- keep relationship or memory semantics local to one world;
- retain a bounded Agent-created Artifact path while rejecting arbitrary tool construction;
- separate product and research builds more strongly;
- defer social or habitat worlds;
- expand only after a second world reproduces value;
- delete the broad hypothesis if Agents add no distinct experiential value.

## Non-goals

This question does not authorize:

- delaying the Station Zero first playable;
- building a general-purpose engine or world platform;
- creating hundreds of autonomous residents;
- treating many-Agent dialogue as society;
- making all content model-generated;
- arbitrary Agent code execution;
- a universal memory, relationship, institution, or narrative schema;
- moving Game semantics into Host or Protocol;
- making Security Campaign objects universal Game state;
- replacing authored aesthetic judgment with emergence;
- promoting an abstraction from Station Zero alone.

## Repository responsibilities

### Ordivon Game

- owns product judgment, world content, experience, Agent-visible domain semantics, and the first bounded experiment;
- preserves the authoritative World, replay, and player surface;
- compares Agent participation with authored scripted baselines;
- localizes or deletes mechanisms that do not produce experiential value.

### Ordivon Computing

- owns the comparative research question, source synthesis, cross-project boundary, evidence criteria, and promotion test;
- prevents Game research utility from replacing Game's intrinsic product purpose;
- requires a second world before shared abstraction promotion.

### Ordivon Host

- continues to own generic durable Goal, Task, Context, Harness coordination, Effect, Dispatch, Observation, Verification, Outcome, and recovery semantics;
- does not absorb game-specific memory, relationship, social, narrative, or scheduling rules without cross-workload evidence.

### Ordivon Security

- may consume Game worlds for adversarial experiments;
- owns Campaign, opponent, deception, and evaluation meaning;
- does not determine the complete Game roadmap or require every experience to be adversarial.
