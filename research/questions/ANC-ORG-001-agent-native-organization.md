# ANC-ORG-001 — Plural-Intelligence Organization

## Question

How should people, artificial participants, multiple models, Tools, institutions, resource owners, and persistent project state coordinate around long-running Goals without making conversation history, fixed role charts, permanent human supremacy, or centralized approval the primary organizational structure?

## Current hypothesis

An Agent-native collaboration system can organize around six planes:

```text
Participants and commitments
Goal and open work
Capability and consequence
Execution
Knowledge and evidence
Feedback and adaptation
```

The shared operational objects are Goals, Tasks, Attempts, Workspaces, Action Proposals, Effects, Artifacts, Facts, Commitments, and bounded continuation records.

Current people and organizations provide much of the legal authority, physical resource ownership, social context, and value judgment. Models and Agents provide exploration, proposal, execution, verification, and continuity. The architecture should represent those current relationships exactly without freezing one intelligence form as the permanent owner of all future purpose.

## Organizational principles

1. **Identity before hierarchy** — participants and institutions have explicit identities and resource relationships; a role does not imply unlimited authority.
2. **Open proposal before commitment** — cognition may propose new actions, subgoals, Tools, and verification methods before deterministic Effect compilation.
3. **Capability separate from consequence** — broad reversible exploration does not grant authority over shared or irreversible effects.
4. **Responsibility routing** — DecisionRequests go to the participant, verifier, resource owner, or institution that can actually accept the missing commitment.
5. **Refusal and exit** — coordination can represent rejection, revocation, transfer, and departure rather than assuming permanent obedience.
6. **Evidence-backed joins** — collaboration converges through Artifacts, Claims, Verification, and explicit integration rules rather than hidden transcript consensus.
7. **Constraint deletion** — approvals, compatibility paths, and governance objects remain only while their measured benefit exceeds their friction and concentration cost.

## First experiment

Use one real cross-project Goal with several genuinely independent branches:

- code or architecture inspection;
- empirical test execution;
- literature or external-system research;
- synthesis and integration.

Run the branches through separate Workspaces or Agents and join them through Artifacts rather than copied conversation histories. At least one branch must generate an ActionProposal not pre-enumerated by the Host. At least one consequential decision must route to its actual resource or commitment owner rather than to a universal human-approval queue.

## Evidence

- useful parallel progress rather than duplicated work;
- lower time to first useful Artifact;
- low-cost handoff through a bounded continuation record;
- clear state after one model, Agent, or Host process is replaced;
- successful Artifact-based Join;
- an open ActionProposal is compiled, revised, or rejected without a fixed action menu;
- DecisionRequest routing identifies the correct responsibility owner;
- refusal, revocation, or redirection does not destroy work continuity;
- active participant attention falls relative to approve-every-action;
- no participant can silently claim another participant’s resources or authority.

## Falsifiers and deletion tests

- If open ActionProposal lowering produces no better outcomes than exact CandidateAction menus, retain the bounded path and reject generalization.
- If a generic Commitment or Participant protocol does not transfer across two materially different domains, keep the semantics product-local.
- If a DecisionRequest object creates more interruption and state duplication than it removes, delete it.
- If multi-Agent structure only adds model budget, latency, or duplicate work, simplify toward fewer actors.

## Boundaries

This research does not decide consciousness, legal personhood, political legitimacy, or universal rights. It does not allow a model to self-authorize access to another party’s resources. It constructs the minimum durable coordination structure required to represent current ownership accurately while remaining compatible with future cooperation among unequal forms of intelligence.
