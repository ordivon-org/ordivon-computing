# Adversarial Review — Draft 0.1

> **Editorial status after Draft 0.3:** this review correctly identified logical gaps, but Draft 0.2 over-promoted those objections into a neutral policy position. Draft 0.3 retains the logical corrections while restoring the article's intended historical and accelerationist thesis. The review is evidence against sloppy reasoning, not a veto on strong judgment.

## 1. Review standard

The review treats the article as a claim that must survive the strongest available alternative, not as a manifesto whose conclusion is fixed in advance.

Each finding asks:

```text
What would a well-informed pacing advocate attack?
Is the attack factual, logical, empirical, or normative?
Does it require deleting the thesis, narrowing it, or adding evidence?
```

## 2. Critical findings

### F1 — Impossibility of slowdown does not imply optimality of capability acceleration

**Problem:** Draft 0.1 moved too quickly from “frontier-wide slowdown is unstable” to “accelerate scientific and technical capability.” This is a non sequitur. A policy can be difficult to enforce while still being preferable if partial compliance materially reduces expected catastrophe. Conversely, a governance failure does not prove that maximizing capability growth is welfare-improving.

**Correction:** Separate three claims:

1. comprehensive global suppression is not currently demonstrated to be stable or verifiable;
2. selective, observable, temporary restrictions can still be rational;
3. adaptive acceleration is a positive strategy because it raises verification, defense, recovery, diffusion, and institutional capacity—not merely because pacing is difficult.

**Status:** corrected in Draft 0.2.

### F2 — “Govern effects, not capability” is too categorical

**Problem:** Some dangerous capabilities are dual-use and can lower the cost of catastrophic action before a concrete external effect occurs. Compute thresholds, secure-development obligations, model evaluations, and access restrictions can be legitimate upstream controls.

**Correction:** Prefer governance of concrete effects and observable enabling bottlenecks, while allowing capability-triggered obligations when a threshold is measurable, risk-linked, revisable, and proportionate. Reject only the stronger claim that present institutions should permanently own the total rate of intelligence development.

**Status:** corrected in Draft 0.2.

### F3 — The game-theoretic model is under-specified

**Problem:** A two-player one-shot matrix can illustrate defection pressure but cannot establish impossibility. Repeated interaction, monitoring, side payments, clubs, trade sanctions, certification, security guarantees, and reciprocal escalation can support cooperation.

**Correction:** Treat the matrix as a heuristic. The article now asks whether a specific pacing regime changes payoffs, detects material defection, covers important actors, survives entry, and remains legitimate. Compute governance and frontier auditing are serious mechanisms, not dismissed as aspirations [G01][G02][G05].

**Status:** corrected in Draft 0.2.

## 3. High-severity findings

### F4 — Historical survivor bias

**Problem:** Steam, electricity, and computing are selected partly because they became successful general-purpose technologies. Historical cases of harmful products or practices being phased out—ozone-depleting substances, leaded fuels, specific weapons, unsafe medicines—show that societies can suppress or redirect technology.

**Correction:** The article now states that industrial history supports mechanisms of diffusion, complementarity, and competition; it does not prove every technology should survive. The relevant comparison is between general-purpose knowledge production and identifiable harmful substances, products, or effects.

### F5 — AI observability was understated

**Problem:** Frontier training still depends on concentrated semiconductor supply chains, large clusters, cloud providers, power, and network infrastructure. Compute is detectable, excludable, and quantifiable enough to support reporting, KYC, thresholds, export controls, and some treaty verification [G01][G02]. New 2026 work explores research verification and hardware mechanisms [G03][G04].

**Correction:** Replace “AI cannot be verified” with a layered claim:

```text
large training runs may be partially observable
specific compute and deployment interfaces may be governable
algorithmic, organizational, distributed, and downstream progress remains harder to inventory
no current mechanism verifies the entire cognition frontier
```

### F6 — Expected-loss reasoning was missing

**Problem:** If a plausible failure has extremely large irreversible loss, even an incomplete slowdown can have positive expected value. Competition and leakage do not by themselves defeat this argument.

**Correction:** Add a decision criterion:

```text
net pacing value
= risk reduction from delayed capability and deployment
- risk added by delayed defense and adaptation
- evasion, concentration, and leadership-composition effects
- enforcement and legitimacy costs
```

The article cannot resolve this expression without empirical estimates. It can require pacing proposals to make the terms explicit.

### F7 — Adaptive acceleration could reinforce concentration

**Problem:** Faster capability research can strengthen incumbent firms because frontier compute, distribution, and capital are concentrated. General calls for diffusion do not guarantee broad ownership.

**Correction:** Treat access, interoperability, public compute, portability, antitrust, labor adaptation, and ownership as structural components rather than optional social policy.

### F8 — AI moral status is not load-bearing

**Problem:** The non-domination section risks distracting from the empirical thesis and may appear to infer subjecthood from capability.

**Correction:** State explicitly that the article's historical and game-theoretic claims do not depend on AI consciousness or legal personhood. Preserve the long-horizon architecture principle conditionally: if systems later demonstrate morally relevant persistence and interests, tool-only architecture should not make non-domination impossible.

## 4. Strongest remaining pacing case

After correction, the strongest opposing position is:

> Frontier training and deployment are concentrated enough that a coalition of major chip-producing states, cloud providers, model developers, and markets could impose reporting, audits, compute thresholds, security standards, deployment gates, and reciprocal restrictions. Even incomplete compliance could reduce catastrophic risk during a critical transition, and the expected value of delay may dominate economic opportunity costs.

This position is not refuted by saying “innovation always escapes.” It must be tested on:

- coalition coverage;
- supply-chain concentration over time;
- algorithmic substitution;
- distributed and stolen compute;
- verification quality;
- state-level deception;
- open-weight diffusion;
- speed of defensive versus offensive capability;
- risk concentration in incumbents;
- exit and revision rules.

## 5. Editorial resolution in Draft 0.3

The review produced three corrections that remain binding:

1. difficulty of global pacing does not by itself prove that every capability increase is beneficial;
2. concentrated compute and frontier organizations provide real governance levers;
3. repeated games can coordinate narrow, observable actions.

It did **not** establish that Ordivon should become neutral between acceleration and restraint. Draft 0.2 mistakenly allowed the red-team objections to become the article's governing voice.

Draft 0.3 therefore restores the stronger thesis:

> If civilization survives and remains plural, useful general-purpose capabilities will continue to be discovered, copied, recombined, and expanded. Pacing may interrupt a specific trajectory, but it cannot serve as the stable foundation of technological civilization. Ordivon chooses to accelerate intelligence together with verification, defense, recovery, diffusion, institutional learning, and cooperation.

Unanswered questions remain research problems. They are not automatically vetoes on movement.

## 6. Promotion decision

- Article remains **Draft** because it still requires compression and publication editing, not because its position is undecided.
- Ordivon remains centered on faster verified-improvement loops and expanding cooperation.
- No new Core primitive or `AccelerationEnvelope` is justified.
- The next review should attack historical claims and prose precision without converting the article back into a neutral policy paper.
