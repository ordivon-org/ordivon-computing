# Canada

Observed through: 2026-08-01

## Current mix

Canada currently combines voluntary private-sector commitments, public-sector AI
directives and registers, existing privacy and human-rights law, proposed privacy
modernization, procurement, and national AI strategy.

The Voluntary Code of Conduct for advanced generative AI asks signatories to
implement accountability, safety, fairness, transparency, human oversight,
monitoring, robustness, red teaming, cybersecurity, and incident practices. It
is not itself binding law and does not replace existing obligations.

The 2025-2027 federal public-service AI strategy created a Centre of Expertise and
prioritized responsible, secure, transparent adoption. A minimum viable federal
AI Register was published in late 2025.

Canada's June 2026 national strategy emphasizes trust, opportunity, and sovereignty,
including sovereign AI infrastructure, adoption, talent, and alignment with trusted
partners. Bill C-36 introduced in June 2026 proposes private-sector privacy
modernization; it must not be treated as enacted law while still a bill.

## Power analysis

Canada offers relatively strong consultation, public-register, and responsible-
use language but currently relies heavily on voluntary codes and existing law for
frontier Provider governance. Foreign Provider and cloud dependence creates an
exit problem that the sovereignty agenda seeks to reduce.

Sovereign infrastructure can diversify dependency, but centralized public
procurement or preferred partnerships can create new domestic gatekeepers.
