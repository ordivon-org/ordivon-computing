# European Union

Observed through: 2026-08-01

## AI Act

Regulation (EU) 2024/1689 applies broadly from 2026-08-02, with prohibitions,
governance, penalty, and general-purpose AI obligations phased earlier.

For general-purpose AI models, the Act creates duties including technical
and downstream documentation, copyright policy, public training-content summary,
cooperation with authorities, and additional obligations for models classified
as presenting systemic risk.

Systemic-risk providers must evaluate and mitigate risks, document adversarial
testing, report serious incidents, maintain lifecycle risk management, and provide
cybersecurity protection. The Commission may designate a model based on capability
or impact, and providers have a reasoned reassessment route.

Open-source exemptions reduce some ordinary GPAI documentation duties when weights,
architecture, and usage information are publicly available, but do not remove
systemic-risk obligations.

## Digital Services Act

The DSA adds reason-giving, transparency reporting, public statement-of-reasons
data, researcher access, risk assessments, and audits for covered online services.
These mechanisms are relevant where AI products operate as content, search,
marketplace, or platform intermediaries.

## Power analysis

EU law supplies external normative and adjudicative authority that can constrain
Providers. It also gives regulators classification, audit, information, and market-
access power. The regime can improve downstream knowledge and public contestability
while increasing the fixed cost of frontier-model distribution.
