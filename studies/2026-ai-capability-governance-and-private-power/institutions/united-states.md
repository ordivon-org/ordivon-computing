# United States

Observed through: 2026-08-01

## Current federal direction

The January 2025 executive order revoked the prior comprehensive AI executive
order and directed an action plan focused on sustaining American AI leadership.
The July 2025 AI Action Plan organizes federal activity around innovation,
infrastructure, and international diplomacy/security, including export of full-
stack American AI packages.

A June 2026 national-security memorandum further directs AI use and governance
inside the national-security enterprise.

## Governance mode

The United States currently relies on a mix of:

- Provider self-governance and voluntary frameworks;
- procurement and contract controls;
- sector law and agency authority;
- consumer protection, competition, civil-rights and privacy law;
- national-security and classified-use rules;
- chip, compute, cloud, sanctions and export controls;
- state-level legislation and litigation.

This produces high institutional diversity but weaker universal reason-giving and
appeal rights than the EU model.

## Power analysis

US policy can reduce ex ante deployment restriction while increasing state and
Provider power through infrastructure investment, procurement, defense/intelligence
integration, export control, and allied distribution. Frontier Providers become
both commercial actors and national strategic infrastructure.

The central risk is not only private monopoly or state censorship. It is a hybrid
national-security industrial system in which capability access depends on
citizenship, geography, allied status, organization, cloud, and procurement.
