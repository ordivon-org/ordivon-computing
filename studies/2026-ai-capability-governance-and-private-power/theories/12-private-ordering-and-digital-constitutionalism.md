# Private Ordering and Digital Constitutionalism

## Private ordering

Generative-AI Providers govern through contracts, licenses, privacy policies,
usage policies, constitutions, access programs, and product design. These sources
can define allowed use, data treatment, model access, automation rights,
competition restrictions, regional eligibility, monitoring, enforcement, and
appeal before a court or regulator becomes involved.

This is private ordering: legally and technically consequential rules created by
private actors for their services and ecosystems.

## Platformisation risk

Research on generative-AI terms identifies a platformisation pattern in which
model Providers resemble neutral intermediaries while also generating content,
shaping model behavior, and allocating capabilities. The governance problem is
therefore broader than traditional hosting moderation.

## Digital constitutionalism

Digital constitutionalism asks how rights and limits on power should apply when
private digital actors perform functions with public importance. Relevant
principles include:

- legality and rule publication;
- specificity and predictability;
- reason-giving;
- equality and non-arbitrariness;
- necessity and proportionality;
- independent review;
- effective remedy;
- limits on surveillance and retention;
- institutional separation or counter-power.

## Procedural fetishism

An appeal button, policy page, or human-review label can improve process while
leaving the underlying power substantively unconstrained. The study must ask both:

```text
Was the procedure followed?
Was this class of decision justified and proportionate at all?
```

## What this theory explains

- how Terms become operational governance;
- why private rules can have public consequences;
- the difference between transparency, procedure, and substantive limits;
- why contract consent may not represent equal bargaining power.

## What it does not establish

- that public constitutional rules transfer unchanged to private services;
- that all mandatory regulation improves users' position;
- the technical effectiveness of a safety control;
- whether market exit is genuinely available.

## Testable implications

1. Policy and product design jointly allocate capability; Terms analysis alone is
   incomplete.
2. Specific reason statements and external dispute resolution should produce more
   reversals and clearer rule evolution than internal generic appeals.
3. Procedural transparency without substantive review can coexist with persistent
   overreach.
4. Mandatory consumer or platform law may constrain private power more effectively
   than voluntary Provider principles, while also creating compliance barriers.

## Falsifiers

- Provider Terms have no practical effect on access, data, or enforcement;
- procedure-only reforms reliably constrain the scope of permissible automated
  decisions;
- users bargain over core terms on roughly equal footing.
