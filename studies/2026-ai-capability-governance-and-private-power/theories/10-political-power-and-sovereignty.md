# Political Power, Sovereignty, and Constituent Authority

## Core contribution

Lukes's multidimensional account prevents the study from observing only explicit
Provider-user conflicts. AI governance power can appear as:

1. direct decisions — refusal, suspension, access approval, or revocation;
2. agenda control — deciding which capabilities, exceptions, or appeals are even
   available for consideration;
3. preference and expectation formation — users internalizing what questions,
   research methods, or access claims are considered legitimate.

The third dimension is particularly relevant when repeated refusals, policy
uncertainty, or account risk cause researchers to narrow work before submitting a
request.

## Sovereignty analogy

Providers can combine rule creation, observation, enforcement, adjudication, and
infrastructure control. This resembles sovereign functions, but the analogy must
remain bounded:

- Providers lack the general territorial jurisdiction of states;
- users may retain contractual and market exit;
- public law still constrains Providers;
- Providers nevertheless exercise functionally public power where their services
  mediate knowledge, speech, work, or action at scale.

The question is therefore not “is a Provider literally a state?” but “which
state-like functions are exercised, over which resources and actors, with what
counter-power?”

## Constituent authority

A model constitution or Model Spec has at least three layers:

```text
authoring authority
training / implementation authority
interpretive and revision authority
```

Publication increases transparency, but it does not answer who may participate in
revision, who can challenge interpretation, or whether users may select another
constitution without losing capability.

## What this theory explains

- rule-making plus enforcement concentration;
- hidden agenda and unavailable exceptions;
- the difference between formal choice and shaped expectation;
- why policy omission may matter as much as a visible refusal.

## What it does not establish

- that every Provider rule is illegitimate;
- that users have a right to every capability;
- that state governance is less concentrated or more accountable;
- the technical mechanism of a particular intervention.

## Testable implications

1. Provider power is understated if measurement counts only refusals and bans.
2. Researchers exposed to uncertain enforcement will self-limit before explicit
   intervention.
3. User participation in policy revision, independent appeal, and low-cost exit
   should reduce third-dimensional power even if some restrictions remain.
4. A published constitution without external revision or exit remains a form of
   concentrated constituent authority.

## Falsifiers

- users do not alter problem selection or expression under policy uncertainty;
- policy omissions have no measurable effect on capability access;
- independent voice and exit do not change Provider behavior or user utility.
