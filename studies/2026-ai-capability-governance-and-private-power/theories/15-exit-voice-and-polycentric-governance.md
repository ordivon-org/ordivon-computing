# Exit, Voice, Loyalty, and Polycentric Governance

## Exit and voice

Hirschman distinguishes two responses to organizational decline or dissatisfaction:

- **exit** — leave for another organization or product;
- **voice** — attempt to change the organization from within.

Loyalty can delay exit and preserve voice, but it can also sustain dependence.
For AI, real exit requires more than another chat interface. It may require
portability of Task state, Context, Tool contracts, Effects, traces, identity,
data, and workflow integration.

## Exit can weaken voice

The most sensitive and capable users may leave first, reducing internal pressure
for reform and concentrating remaining dependence. Open ecosystems should
therefore be evaluated for both migration and feedback institutions.

## Polycentric governance

Ostrom's institutional work rejects universal claims that either centralized
state control or simple privatization is always necessary. Durable governance can
emerge through nested, mixed institutions with participant rule-making,
monitoring, graduated sanctions, dispute resolution, recognized autonomy, and
higher-level coordination.

Applied to AI, possible centers include:

```text
Provider rules
user and researcher communities
open-source maintainers
cloud and compute operators
independent auditors
standards bodies
courts and regulators
sector institutions
local Host / Runtime owners
```

## What this theory explains

- why nominal competition is insufficient without portability;
- the relationship between migration and internal reform;
- alternatives to Provider monopoly and state monopoly;
- why nested institutions may fit heterogeneous AI risks.

## What it does not establish

- that every AI capability is a common-pool resource;
- that communities can manage catastrophic or cross-border externalities alone;
- that exit is safe for affected third parties;
- the optimal number of governance centers.

## Testable implications

1. Provider-neutral state improves exit and bargaining power.
2. Independent appeals and user participation strengthen voice even where exit is
   costly.
3. Mixed governance can outperform one universal rule across heterogeneous risks.
4. Polycentricity without interoperability or conflict resolution may merely
   fragment power and externalize harm.

## Falsifiers

- state portability does not improve real migration or Provider responsiveness;
- a single centralized institution consistently dominates mixed governance across
  risk, innovation, privacy, and legitimacy;
- participant governance cannot manage even bounded owned-world capability.
