# Governmentality, Classification, and Organizational Risk

## Government through conduct

Governmentality shifts attention from direct prohibition to systems that shape
how actors govern themselves. In AI services, relevant techniques include:

- trusted-user and verified-organization categories;
- risk scores and cumulative account signals;
- retention conditions tied to capability access;
- warnings, friction, and probabilistic review;
- model routing and capability tiers;
- published examples of acceptable conduct;
- uncertain boundaries that encourage precautionary self-editing.

Power can operate through freedom: the user remains able to choose, but the field
of safe, affordable, and account-preserving choices is structured.

## Classification effects

Categories such as “trusted,” “high risk,” “dual use,” “supported region,” or
“verified organization” do not merely describe actors. They allocate capability,
monitoring, evidentiary burden, and appeal pathways.

A category therefore has:

```text
selection rule
observed signals
assigned status
capability consequence
data consequence
review process
expiration or persistence
```

## Organizational risk asymmetry

Provider teams face asymmetric visibility:

```text
one severe false negative
→ concentrated legal, reputational, regulatory, and security loss

many legitimate false positives
→ distributed user cost, abandoned research, and invisible opportunity loss
```

This can produce rational overblocking without malicious intent. Responsibility
may also diffuse across policy, safety, legal, model, infrastructure, and support
teams, making correction difficult even when no actor intends the final outcome.

## What this theory explains

- self-censorship and behavior normalization;
- why identity and retention become access conditions;
- why organizations may prefer precautionary denial;
- why no single employee appears responsible for a restrictive system.

## What it does not establish

- whether a classification is accurate or normatively justified;
- that surveillance is always coercive;
- the market power enabling users to be governed this way;
- the actual rate of false positives or prevented harms.

## Testable implications

1. Users with prior high-risk research adapt future wording and task choice even
   absent a current refusal.
2. Provider incentives weight salient misuse more heavily than dispersed research
   opportunity cost.
3. Capability programs that require retention or identity create more observable
   and administratively governable subjects.
4. Specific reasons and bounded risk-state expiration should reduce precautionary
   self-censorship more than generic warnings.

## Falsifiers

- no behavioral adaptation follows warnings or uncertain thresholds;
- Providers measure and internalize legitimate-denial costs as strongly as misuse;
- risk classes do not alter capability, observation, or data lifecycle.
