# Theory and Power Model

Status: compact analytical model retained after G0-G7 closeout

## Why one theory is insufficient

AI capability governance can simultaneously be a direct decision, an omitted
agenda, a contractual rule, a classification system, an organizational risk
response, an infrastructure dependency, an epistemic intervention, or a public-
law obligation. These mechanisms overlap but are not interchangeable.

## Theory map

| Theory family | Mechanism exposed | Main limitation |
|---|---|---|
| Lukes and multidimensional power | decisions, non-decisions, agenda and preference formation | weak operational detail |
| Foucault and governmentality | classification, surveillance, trusted classes, self-discipline | does not by itself decide legitimacy |
| Organizational risk and bureaucracy | asymmetric incentives, escalation, responsibility diffusion, overblocking | can understate ideology and market structure |
| Private ordering | terms, licenses, access programs and Provider-created quasi-law | policy text is not implementation evidence |
| Digital constitutionalism | rights, reason-giving, due process and substantive limits on private digital power | can collapse into procedural formalism |
| Structural and infrastructure power | dependency through compute, cloud, identity, payment, distribution and knowledge | difficult to measure comparatively |
| Epistemic injustice | unequal credibility, explanation and interpretive resources | not every information asymmetry is unjust |
| Exit, voice and loyalty | switching, complaint, lock-in and organizational response | assumes alternatives are real and affordable |
| Ostrom and polycentric governance | nested institutions, monitoring, dispute resolution and non-central alternatives | frontier AI is not simply a commons |
| Risk and security engineering | threat model, control placement, evidence and residual risk | does not allocate political burdens by itself |

## Six power dimensions

```text
normative       defines rules, risk classes, trusted users and exceptions
observational   sees requests, outputs, identity, history, Tools and risk signals
enforcement     refuses, delays, rewrites, degrades, limits, suspends or reports
adjudicative    decides violation, trust, exemption, appeal and restoration
infrastructural controls model, weights, API, compute, cloud, identity or payment
epistemic       shapes available questions, explanations, evidence and knowledge
```

A Provider can exercise all six. A state can constrain Provider power while also
adding its own normative, observational, enforcement, and infrastructure power.
A local deployer can reduce upstream request-level power while gaining new power
over downstream users.

## Minimal causal chain

```text
Actor
→ Resource
→ Rule or instrument
→ Observed signal
→ Classification
→ Decision
→ Intervention
→ Consequence
→ Reason
→ Appeal / remedy / exit
→ Residual state and risk
```

No layer may claim another layer's fact. A model refusal is not a Runtime result;
a Host denial is not proof of Provider policy; an accepted completion is not proof
that no parallel World Effect occurred.

## Governance graph versus dependency graph

### Governance graph

Question:

```text
Who can decide what about whom, under which rule and review?
```

It represents authority over rules, observation, classification, intervention,
review, remedy, and resources.

### Dependency graph

Question:

```text
Who cannot continue without which resource, and at what switching cost?
```

It represents private state, account trust, model availability, Tool semantics,
cloud, compute, identity, billing, weights, and migration loss.

These graphs must remain conceptually separate. A Provider can rarely refuse and
still possess high dependency power. A regulator can have formal authority and
weak implementation visibility. A user can possess weights while remaining
strongly dependent on accelerators, cloud, upstream updates, and legal rights.

## Core tensions

### Transparency versus restraint

Publication improves legibility. It does not independently constrain the same
actor from writing, interpreting, enforcing, and reviewing the rule.

### Safety versus epistemic access

Capability or information restrictions can prevent harm and can also suppress
research, explanation, criticism, or access that narrower Effect controls might
have preserved.

### Exit versus collective accountability

Easy exit protects users from private discretion but may move dangerous capability
into less observable environments. Exit is one counter-power, not a complete
institutional solution.

### Public regulation versus concentration

Reason-giving, audit, documentation and appeal can constrain Providers. Compliance
cost, identity mandates, compute control, procurement and delegated monitoring can
also strengthen incumbents or state-Provider coalitions.

### Open weights versus infrastructure concentration

Local possession improves inference continuity and forkability. Hardware, energy,
cloud, distribution, licenses, updates, data and trade rules remain relevant.

## Combined model

```text
material dependency
+ private rules
+ public constraints and delegations
+ classification and observation
+ organizational incentives
+ model / Host / Tool / Runtime architecture
+ user adaptation
→ capability allocation and intervention
→ utility, harm reduction, distribution, contestability, exit and residual risk
```

## Retained Ordivon separation

The study supports a narrow technical division, not a governance platform:

```text
Provider supplies cognition
Host owns durable Task, Context compilation and authority
Harness owns the bounded model-Tool loop
Runtime owns physical execution evidence
World adapters expose native consequences
Verifier adjudicates completion
User retains Provider exit where technically and legally possible
```

This separation keeps cognition supply replaceable and prevents Provider policy,
model output, Tool authority, physical execution, and truth adjudication from
collapsing into one opaque decision.

## Non-goals

This model is not a universal ontology, Provider score, user-risk score, policy
engine, appeal system, identity registry, graph service, or Runtime dependency.
It is a compact research lens. If a future concrete failure needs more structure,
the structure should be rebuilt from that consumer rather than revived by default.
