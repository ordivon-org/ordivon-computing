# References

Access date unless otherwise noted: 2026-08-01.

The ledger distinguishes analytical theory, official policy, technical evidence,
public law, aggregate reporting, and controlled observations. Inclusion does not
endorse a source's normative conclusions.

## Political and institutional theory

### [G001] Steven Lukes — *Power: A Radical View*

- Publisher: Bloomsbury Academic, third edition.
- URL: https://www.bloomsbury.com/uk/power-9781352012347/
- Evidence class: E0.
- Supports: direct decision power, agenda control, and preference formation as
  distinct analytical dimensions.

### [G002] Michel Foucault — *Security, Territory, Population*

- Publisher: Palgrave Macmillan.
- DOI: https://doi.org/10.1057/9780230245075
- Evidence class: E0.
- Supports: governmentality, security techniques, population management, and
  governing conduct through structured freedom.

### [G003] Albert O. Hirschman — *Exit, Voice, and Loyalty*

- Publisher: Harvard University Press, 1970.
- URL: https://www.hup.harvard.edu/books/9780674276604
- Evidence class: E0.
- Supports: exit and voice as responses to organizational decline, with loyalty
  mediating their use.

### [G004] Miranda Fricker — *Epistemic Injustice: Power and the Ethics of Knowing*

- Publisher: Oxford University Press, 2007.
- URL: https://academic.oup.com/book/32817
- Evidence class: E0.
- Supports: testimonial and hermeneutical injustice and the relationship between
  social power and epistemic participation.

### [G005] Elinor Ostrom — *Governing the Commons*

- Publisher: Cambridge University Press, 1990.
- URL: https://www.cambridge.org/core/books/governing-the-commons/7AB7AE11BADA84409C34815CC288CD79
- Evidence class: E0.
- Supports: institutional diversity, self-organization, monitoring, dispute
  resolution, and rejection of universal state/private panaceas.

### [G006] Susan Strange — *States and Markets*

- Publisher: Bloomsbury Academic.
- URL: https://www.bloomsbury.com/ca/states-and-markets-9781474236935/
- Evidence class: E0.
- Supports: structural power in security, production, finance, and knowledge.

## Digital constitutionalism and private ordering

### [G007] Nicolas P. Suzor and coauthors — Digital due process

- Source: *The Oxford Handbook of Digital Constitutionalism*.
- URL: https://academic.oup.com/edited-volume/58210/chapter-abstract/529884533
- Evidence class: E0.
- Supports: the distinction between procedural safeguards and substantive limits,
  including the risk of procedural fetishism.

### [G008] Gunther Teubner and constitutionalist scholarship — Societal constitutionalism

- Source: *The Oxford Handbook of Digital Constitutionalism*.
- URL: https://academic.oup.com/edited-volume/58210
- Evidence class: E0.
- Supports: constitutional restraints beyond the nation-state and application to
  private and transnational digital power.

### [G009] Edoardo Celeste and related scholarship — Constitutionalizing platform governance

- Source: *The Oxford Handbook of Digital Constitutionalism*.
- URL: https://academic.oup.com/edited-volume/58210/chapter-abstract/488307363
- Evidence class: E0.
- Supports: platform governance as an object of rights, legitimacy, and power
  limitation rather than only contract administration.

### [G010] Lilian Edwards et al. — Private ordering and the platformisation paradigm

- Journal: *Cambridge Forum on AI: Law and Governance*, 2025, e2.
- DOI: https://doi.org/10.1017/cfl.2024.11
- URL: https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/cambridge-forum-on-ai-law-and-governance/article/private-ordering-generative-ai-and-the-platformisation-paradigm-what-can-we-learn-from-comparative-analysis-of-models-terms-and-conditions/92790919A0203140ED012BF8A4BA8A0F
- Evidence class: E0/E5.
- Supports: generative-AI providers governing through terms, licenses, and privacy
  policies and the need for versioned comparative policy datasets.

### [G011] Platform power and regulatory capture in digital governance

- Journal: *Business and Politics*.
- URL: https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/business-and-politics/article/platform-power-and-regulatory-capture-in-digital-governance/EBA8E383142780A36EE4F468A9BFCDAC
- Evidence class: E0/E5.
- Supports: firms shaping not only market outcomes but the regulatory environment
  governing digital infrastructure.

## Compute, cloud, and risk engineering

### [G012] Lennart Heim et al. — Computing Power and the Governance of Artificial Intelligence

- arXiv: 2402.08797.
- URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.08797
- Evidence class: E0/E2.
- Supports: compute as a detectable, excludable, quantifiable, and concentrated
  governance point, together with privacy and concentration tradeoffs.

### [G013] Governing Through the Cloud

- arXiv: 2403.08501.
- URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2403.08501
- Evidence class: E0/E2.
- Supports: compute providers as regulatory intermediaries and the expansion of
  enforcement points beyond model developers.

### [G014] NIST AI Risk Management Framework 1.0

- DOI: https://doi.org/10.6028/NIST.AI.100-1
- URL: https://www.nist.gov/publications/artificial-intelligence-risk-management-framework-ai-rmf-10
