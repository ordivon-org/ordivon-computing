# Source Ledger

This ledger separates external observations from Ordivon's derived claims. First-party reports establish what their authors report about systems and experiments; they do not independently prove every broader inference in this study.

## OpenAI

### O01 — Ten advances in mathematics and theoretical computer science

- Organization: OpenAI
- Date: 2026-08-01
- URL: https://openai.com/index/ten-advances-in-mathematics/
- Supports: OpenAI's report that an internal Astra model generated arguments for ten long-open problems, humans prepared manuscripts with the model, and Lean certificates and reasoning walkthroughs were released.
- Does not by itself support: final community acceptance, journal peer review, universal autonomous-research capability, or the importance of every result.

### O02 — Scientific computing in the age of agentic AI

- Organization: OpenAI
- Date: 2026-07-28
- URL: https://openai.com/index/scientific-computing-agentic-ai/
- Supports: eight exploratory scientific-software cases; lower engineering cost; role movement toward specification, validation, orchestration, and stewardship; persistent last-mile and maintenance problems.
- Does not by itself support: measured economy-wide productivity effects or replacement of scientific expertise.

### O03 — How enabling two settings tripled our scores on the ARC-AGI-3 benchmark

- Organization: OpenAI
- Date: 2026-07-29
- URL: https://openai.com/index/how-two-settings-tripled-our-arc-agi-3-scores/
- Supports: reported public-set score change from 13.3% to 38.3% with retained reasoning and compaction, and approximately sixfold lower output tokens; claim that benchmark results depend on Harness and API configuration.
- Does not by itself support: general performance multipliers for all models, tasks, or Harnesses.

### O04 — Early experiments in accelerating science with GPT-5

- Organization: OpenAI
- Date: 2025-11-20
- URL: https://openai.com/index/accelerating-science-gpt-5/
- Supports: curated cases of model-assisted literature search, proof work, counterexamples, mechanism proposals, and experiment design; explicit limitations and expert-oversight requirements.
- Does not by itself support: representative average acceleration or autonomous project completion.

## Google DeepMind

### G01 — AlphaEvolve: A Gemini-powered coding agent for designing advanced algorithms

- Organization: Google DeepMind
- Date: 2025-05-14
- URL: https://deepmind.google/blog/alphaevolve-a-gemini-powered-coding-agent-for-designing-advanced-algorithms/
- Supports: a system combining model-generated program candidates, automated evaluators, a program database, and evolutionary selection for algorithm discovery and optimization.
- Does not by itself support: suitability for domains without reliable executable evaluators.

### G02 — Conjecture Machines: AI agents and the new validation bottleneck in science

- Organization: Google DeepMind
- Date: 2026-07
- URL: https://deepmind.google/public-policy/conjecture-machines-ai-agents-and-the-new-validation-bottleneck-in-science/
- Supports: the framing that Agent systems can make hypotheses and candidates abundant while physical and institutional validation remains scarce; policy discussion of datasets, peer review, and automated laboratories.
- Does not by itself support: a complete economic model of science or inevitability of autonomous discovery.
